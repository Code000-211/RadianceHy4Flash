# F-16: sink super-chunks at window 0 / sel (`kShiftFull` 7 -> 6)

LOSSY, opt-in (`notes/backlog/CARDS.md` §3). The switch is `RADIANCE_HY4_SINK_SC=64`, off by default:
unset (or any value but 64) is today's law, byte for byte.

## What the switch trades

`mla_attn_paged_sink` at window 0 and `mla_attn_sel_sink` cut a row's range into super-chunks of 128
tokens (`kShiftFull`, `libr4d/r4d_mla_sink.hip`), 20 of them over the law's 2560-token table, each
walked in 16-token chunks and folded into the row's accumulator in slot order. A decode row's time is
the longest slot's chunks, so half the super-chunk halves that walk; the same range then has 40 slots
and twice the partials' scratch (126 MB a card at 32 heads x 48 rows, half at TP2).

Every boundary of the 128-token law is a boundary of the 64-token law, so the switched run selects
the same keys and only the fold's association changes: the output moves in its last bits. In the
emulator (`tests/r4d_emu/test_mla_sink.cpp`, `check_sink_sc`) 32 of 24576 values move, at most
1.2e-4; the launch stays inside the f64 reference's bound (7.5e-4, unchanged), the paged form at
window 0 still equals the selection form byte for byte, a row's bytes are its own, and a window's
launches do not move at all. What that costs a served model's distribution is the KL below.

The switch's other cases in the emulator are short ranges (1100 tokens at most), where slot 17 is
the last one that holds a key and the second of the merge's two batches -- slots 20..39 -- only ever
skips empty slots; an offset or a fold wrong in it would have corrupted every window-0 row longer
than 1280 tokens with no test failing. Two of the cases are not short: at 1400 tokens (slots 0..21
hold keys, 20 and 21 in the second batch) and at 2700 (the tail slot takes [2496, 2700), past the
law's 2560-token table), each checked against the f64 reference in the split and the fused form and
against the selection form (max_abs 4.66e-4 and 3.73e-4, both inside the bound). A wrong offset in
that batch passes every case the set had and fails these.

## The default law's rows, on a card

`scripts/kbench-fixtures.sh` (the recorded answers of the reference library, replayed against this
build on one R9700) passes every recording: `kernels_hy4.rkb` 2967 checked, 2967 passed, 0 failed
(worst rel_l2 3.183e-03; its 12 `diverged` rows are the stale `qsa_tail_store` output descriptions,
not correctness results, and none of them is a sink), and `kernels.rkb` 2172/2172,
`kernels_moe.rkb` 1863/1863, `kernels_nemotronh.rkb` 2845/2845, 0 failed. That is the law the switch
leaves alone: no recorded sink row moved a bit. The switched law has no recording (the fixtures are
`data/*.rkb`, a locked path), so its cost stays what the emulator measured and what the KL below
owns.

The merge kernel's batch is 20 slots, and the widest law's second batch is short of that, so a slot
past the batch used to be a register LLVM read before writing it (the RDNA4 ISA suite's
`UNINITIALIZED_READ`, whose count a review watched grow from 18 to 30) and an address past the row's
partials. A dead slot now holds `-INFINITY` / 0 / 0, which the fold skips on its denominator exactly
as before, so no byte moves: `bash check-kernel.sh libr4d/r4d_mla_sink.hip` reports 0 errors and 0
warnings at the head (it reported 12 of them before this fix), and the fixtures above still replay
clean.

## Measured end to end on one lane

**These six runs are of the head `5653ec40`'s code (the tree served was the notes-only commit
`2321c45a` on it), not of this PR's head.** After them the merge kernel gained one thing: a dead slot
of `merge_sink_slots` is written (`-INFINITY` / 0 / 0) instead of being read before it is written.
The fold skips that slot on its denominator exactly as it did, so no byte moves -- the emulator's
count is the same 32 of 24576 values at the same 1.22e-4 and the replay of the recorded fixtures is
clean at the head -- but the record's after commit is not the head, and a push makes the number of
an old head count for nothing. So the rows below say what the switch does and are not the head's
timing: **the head's own runs are owed** (`## Still owed`).

Before = the merge base `e3834473`; after = `2321c45a`, the notes-only commit this file's PR carried

`RADIANCE_HY4_SINK_SC` unset (A) and `=64` (B) are two runs of the same binary, alternating

The kernel the item targets (`mla_sink_rows`, the sink's row writer) is **-2.2 us a launch (-12.5%),
-90 us a step**; the merge that has twice the slots to fold pays **+1.4 us a launch (+18%), +60 us a
step**, so the pair is **-31 us a step, -2.9% of itself and -0.24% of the 13.2 ms step**. The backlog
estimated 0.04 ms; the measured saving is 0.031 ms.

Note what these numbers are not: they are at a 73-token context, where the selection form still cuts
its whole 2048-token block range (`kLawTable` in the selection form), which is why the 64-token law
gains there at all. At a long window-0 context both laws stop at the same 2560-token table
(`kLawTable = 20 * 128`) with the last slot taking the rest, so the window-0 side of the gain shrinks
with context and only the selection form keeps it.
