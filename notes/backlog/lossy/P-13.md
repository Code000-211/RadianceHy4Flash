## What the switch does (and does not) change

- **Switch:** `RADIANCE_HY4_ROUTER_WMMA` (`arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash_common.h`, `router_wmma_on()`), **off by default**.
  Any value but `0`/empty turns it on. It reaches `MoeFP8::Config::router_wmma` (`arch/common/rad_block_moe_fp8.h`) and
  `GlmRouter::Config::wmma` (`arch/common/rad_block_glm_router.h`).
- **Every prefill path of the projection moves.** Two ops carry the router's prefill rows, and the switch moves whichever
  of them serves them (both declare `gemm_nt_f32_wmma`, row `gemm_nt_f32_bf16_wmma`, when the switch is on, the router
  weight is stored bf16, K is 64..16384 and a multiple of 64, and the columns are 16..512):
  - the **column split's part** (`SplitF32Proj::declare`, `arch/common/rad_glm_tp.h`) where the split is set up at
    world 2 or more;
  - otherwise the **replicated projection's own prefill band** (`GlmRouter::declare`,
    `arch/common/rad_block_glm_router.h`), which is the prefill path at one rank and at a world the expert count cannot
    split into whole 16-column slices. It is declared as a second op over `M` 256..`max_tok`, with `gemm_nt_f32` left on
    the band below it.
  Both bands' `M` range starts at `GlmTp::kSplitRows` (256), so nothing below the prefill row count is declared, and the
  band is declared only where its rows can occur: not at world 2 when the split already takes them.
- **Every decode row, and every verify step shorter than 256 rows, keeps today's kernel.** The rows below the floor run
  `gemm_nt_f32` from `M` 1, whatever the switch says (`tests/hyv4flash_arch_test.cpp`, case
  `p13_the_prefill_router_takes_the_wmma_row_only_when_asked`: a decode step issues no tile row at all, a 256-row
  prefill step issues it for the bf16-stored router and `gemm_nt_f32` for the f32-stored one). With the switch off the
  graph is byte-identical: the off path declares the same ops, parameters and ranges as before, so the graph hash does
  not move.
- **What is lost:** the summation order of `K`, on prefill router logits only. The operands are the same bf16
  activations and the same stored bf16 weights, the accumulation is f32 in both kernels, and the output is f32 in both
  (`docs/OPS.md`, `gemm_nt_f32_wmma`). bf16 x bf16 is exact in f32, so the products are identical; the tile sums each
  16-deep k step as one WMMA and adds the steps, where `gemm_nt_f32` has lane `l` accumulate `k = l, l+32, ...` with
  fused multiply-add and then a fixed butterfly. The result can differ in the last bits, so a near-tie expert pick or a
  near-tie mixture weight can flip. It is not a precision reduction: no lower accumulation type, no fast-math, no
  approximation.

## Tolerance the branch checks against

`(K + 16) * 2^-24 * sum_j |x_j * w_j|` around the exact (f64) value, for both orders
(`tests/r4d_emu/test_f32.cpp` `tile_cases`, `libr4d/r4d_selftest.cpp` `wmma_forms`). Why that one: each of the K
terms enters one f32 add at a time, and a f32 add rounds at most `2^-24` of the magnitude it joins, so no reordering of
K terms can move the sum further than that bound; the `+16` covers the WMMA's internal 16-deep tree. For the router's
prefill shape (K = 4096, unit-normal activations and weights) that is a relative bound of about `4096 * 6e-8 = 2.4e-4`
of the magnitude sum, which is far below the separations of real router scores except at ties. What KL, perplexity or
top-1 that costs is exactly what the lane's keptn run below measures; the branch does not claim a number.

## The column split after the change (the backlog's "re-check the gather")

The switch changes the *part's* kernel, not the split: the part is still `[rows, experts / world]` plus the
row-interleaved `all_gather`. What may no longer pay is the split itself, because the tile's cost is per 128-column
block: at TP2 the router is 288 experts, which is 3 column blocks unsplit but 2 x 144 split across the two ranks (2
blocks a rank), plus the gather of `rows * 288 * 4` bytes (2 MB at 2048 rows). Whether that trade still pays is a lane
measurement of the two graphs, not something this branch can see.

What the branch now gives the lane is both sides of it: with the switch on, world 1 runs the replicated projection on
the tile (every expert's columns, no gather) and world 2 runs the split part on the tile (half the columns a rank, plus
the gather). **Not measured here: the lane should run the router step with `RADIANCE_HY4_ROUTER_WMMA=1` at world 2 and
at world 1 and compare the ms** -- both are the tile now, so the difference is the split and its gather against the wider
single-rank projection, which is what the item asks. Compare against the same two worlds with the switch off to see
what the tile changed on each. If the split stops paying with the tile, the follow-up is to keep the projection
replicated and skip `all_gather` for prefill rows, which is a separate change (it changes the graph at world 2, not just
the part's row).
