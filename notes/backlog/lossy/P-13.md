## What the switch does (and does not) change

- **Switch:** `RADIANCE_HY4_ROUTER_WMMA` (`arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash_common.h`, `router_wmma_on()`), **off by default**.
  Any value but `0`/empty turns it on. It reaches `MoeFP8::Config::router_wmma` (`arch/common/rad_block_moe_fp8.h`) and
  `GlmRouter::Config::wmma` (`arch/common/rad_block_glm_router.h`).
- **Only the column-split prefill part moves.** `SplitF32Proj::declare` (`arch/common/rad_glm_tp.h`) declares
  `gemm_nt_f32_wmma` (row `gemm_nt_f32_bf16_wmma`) for the part when the switch is on, the world is 2 or more, the
  router weight is stored bf16, K is 64..16384 and a multiple of 64, and the part has 16..512 columns. Its `M` range
  starts at `GlmTp::kSplitRows` (256), so no band below the prefill row count is declared.
- **Every decode and verify row keeps today's kernel.** The unsplit projection of the same weight stays
  `gemm_nt_f32` with `M` from 1, whatever the switch says (`tests/hyv4flash_arch_test.cpp`, case
  `p13_the_prefill_router_takes_the_wmma_row_only_when_asked`). With the switch off the graph is byte-identical: the
  off path declares the same ops, parameters and ranges as before, so the graph hash does not move.
- **What is lost:** the summation order of `K`, on prefill router logits only. The operands are the same bf16
  activations and the same stored bf16 weights, the accumulation is f32 in both kernels, and the output is f32 in both
  (`docs/OPS.md`, `gemm_nt_f32_wmma`). bf16 x bf16 is exact in f32, so the products are identical; the tile sums each
  16-deep k step as one WMMA and adds the steps, where `gemm_nt_f32` has lane `l` accumulate `k = l, l+32, ...` with
  fused multiply-add and then a fixed butterfly. The result can differ in the last bits, so a near-tie expert pick or a
  near-tie mixture weight can flip. It is not a precision reduction: no lower accumulation type, no fast-math, no
  approximation.

## Tolerance the branch checks against

`(K + 16) * 2^-24 * sum_j |x_j * w_j|` around the exact (f64) value, for both orders
(`tests/r4d_emu/test_f32.cpp` `tile_cases`, `libr4d/r4d_selftest.cpp` `wmma_forms`). Why that one: each of the K
terms enters one f32 add at a time, and a f32 add rounds at most `2^-24` of the magnitude it joins, so no reordering of
K terms can move the sum further than that bound; the `+16` covers the WMMA's internal 16-deep tree. For the router's
prefill shape (K = 4096, unit-normal activations and weights) that is a relative bound of about `4096 * 6e-8 = 2.4e-4`
of the magnitude sum, which is far below the separations of real router scores except at ties. What KL, perplexity or
top-1 that costs is exactly what the lane's keptn run below measures; the branch does not claim a number.

## The column split after the change (the backlog's "re-check the gather")

The switch changes the *part's* kernel, not the split: the part is still `[rows, experts / world]` plus the
row-interleaved `all_gather`. What may no longer pay is the split itself, because the tile's cost is per 128-column
block: at TP2 the router is 288 experts, which is 3 column blocks unsplit but 2 x 144 split across the two ranks (2
blocks a rank), plus the gather of `rows * 288 * 4` bytes (2 MB at 2048 rows). Whether that trade pays is a lane
measurement of the two graphs, not something this branch can see. **Not measured here; the lane should run the router
step with `RADIANCE_HY4_ROUTER_WMMA=1` at world 2 and at world 1 (no split) and compare the ms.** If the split stops
paying with the tile, the follow-up is to keep the projection replicated for the switch's case, which is a separate
change (it changes the graph at world 2, not just the part's row).
