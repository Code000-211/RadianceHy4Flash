# D-07 (draft only the rounds the depth rule can take): CLOSED -- the draft pass gets 0.38 ms a step cheaper, but the chain cut costs 13 % of the tokens a step at c1, so DFly2 c1 tok/s falls 9 %

Item as written (backlog D-07, issue #92): the depth rule cannot take draft j + 1 once its statistic fails at j, yet
rounds j + 2..5 run their head pass and correction; either a device flag a round, or (cheaper) a per-step `rounds`
operand from the previous step's depth distribution. Gain DFly2 c1 1-2 %, confidence medium. Verify: tokens a step
and acceptance unchanged; step time.

## What was built (PR #211)

Option B, "decided + 1": `Scheduler::chain_rounds` stages a step's chain at one round past the deepest row the depth
rule decided on the **previous** step (floored by `--draft-depth-min` and the drafter's shortest block, capped by the
head's depth), a new additive export `rad_drafter_any_block` lets a BLOCK drafter serve every block length, DFly2's
query pass declares its anchor and per-round row iotas for every block 2..6, and an exploring step
(`--draft-depth-explore`) keeps the whole block. Code commits `a37c8055`, `99beb98a`, `2c7d461b`, `ea9b4871`,
`0d821b85`, `181d8222`, `7f4086f9`; measured as `7060ab8e` (that head merged with the base `96569ca9`). The code is
clean and tested on CPU (`sched_test`, `build_test`, `hyv4flash_arch_test`, `engine_block_shape_test` pass;
`chain_scalar_audit.py --check` 0 of 2420), and greedy output is byte-identical. It does not merge because of what
it does to tokens a step.

## Why it closes (CARDS.md §2)

1. Output: byte-identical (greedy) -- holds.
2. The targeted pass is faster: the c1 draft pass is **4.945 -> 4.562 ms a step** (median of three; the ranges do not
   overlap, 4.900-4.988 against 4.531-4.591); per pass 4.5-4.7 ms -> 4.3-4.4 ms. After the change the c1 draft
   passes are 48 at five rounds, 29 at four, 8 at three (of 85) instead of all five. At c8 the max over eight rows'
   decisions almost always keeps five rounds (71-78 of 85 passes), so the saving there is small (-0.16 ms). Holds.
3. End to end: **fails at c1.** The chain cut is a ratchet: a step can verify at most `decided + 1` rounds, the
   rule cannot decide deeper than the rounds it was given, so after a short decision the depth climbs back one round
   a step, and every step on that climb verifies a shorter prefix than the rule would have taken. On the c1 request
   that is 84 steps for 256 tokens against 73 (tok/step 3.048 against 3.507, -13 %), in all three after-runs
   identically. The step is 3.9 % cheaper (the draft rounds and the shorter verify), but 15 % more steps make c1
   **132.7 -> 120.6 tok/s (-9.1 %)**. The after-runs agree to 0.6 % (120.1-120.8); two of three before-runs sit at
   132.7; the third (A1, 118.8 tok/s, the first run of the series, slower steps at 25.85 ms) is 1.5 % below them,
   but even it needed fewer steps (80) than every after-run (84). c8 is inside
   noise (-1.1 %, ranges overlap).

The item's own Verify ("tokens a step unchanged") is the clause that fails; the PR body named exactly this risk
("a step whose rule grows by two rounds or more verifies the shorter prefix"). The measured saving (0.38 ms of a
25 ms step, 1.5 %) is the item's predicted size; the token cost is an order of magnitude larger.

## What a re-proposal would need

- Option A of the item -- stop the chain **on the card** (a device flag a round, skip forms of the head GEMM and
  the three correction GEMMs), decided from this step's own statistic, so the rule still sees every round it would
  take and tokens a step cannot change. The per-step operand from the past cannot do that.
- Or an option-B variant whose cap never binds the rule on the steps that matter: e.g. drop the cap entirely after
  any step whose decision reached its cap (the rule wanted more), so the chain returns to the full block in one step
  instead of one round a step. It must show tok/step equal to base on c1 within the run-to-run spread before its
  step saving counts.
