**Item:** M-04 Stager ring sizing and heavy layers (overflow is read in place), issue #116.
**Verdict:** not merged; the change's two code commits (`1595eaab`, `00542790`) are reverted by

## The item as written

- Change `core/mem/vram_budget.cpp` (ring = 2 x ceil(f x layer_max), one global f = 523 MiB there) and
  `core/place/stager.cpp:227` (a layer above ring/2 is not staged ahead; what does not fit is
  `stage_overflow`, `mover.cpp:636-642`), to (1) stage a heavy layer partially ahead, (2) size the
  ring from the per-layer pooled maximum at the KV floor, bounded ~1 GiB, (3) order the pool
  hottest-first so the overflow is the coldest.
- Why, from the audit (M, `res/nospec.json`): overflow 0 at one session, 49 -> 409 units a chunk at
  4 x 32K -> 60K, 819-950 units (1.2-1.4 GiB) a chunk at 8 sessions; read in place, u3 gate_up is
  re-read per 64-row entry (K-44).
- Verify: `/stats experts stage_overflow` per chunk in the longctx sweep; prefill tok/s.
- Merge bar: byte-identical or inside the bound, the targeted op measurably faster, no e2e
  regression beyond noise. Otherwise this file and no code.

## What was built, and what it measured

Built, unit-tested and committed as the two commits above: `place/heat.h` (`heat_of()`), a
`StagePart` so a two-part stage still counts one routed layer, `stage_units` selecting by heat and
then packing in pool order (coalesced DMA, coldest left in place), `Stager::top_up`/`take(...,
reserve, ...)`, a `before_op` order of gate -> retire -> top up -> wait -> stage ahead, and the ring
sized from the heaviest routed layer with a 512 MiB half bound (`kStageBound`). Two new tests in
`tests/place_test.cpp` were red on the base and green on the change: a layer heavier than the ring's
half goes out in two parts without losing its place, and a partial stage of a layer is its hottest
units.

Then the A/B on the local lane, 8 sessions x ~46K prompt tokens (`nospec.yaml`), base `34862f3d`
against the change, alternating, plus one pair at `max-num-seqs: 16` to get a placement with 41 %
expert residency, the one closest to the audit's.

**The counter the item asks for is 0 everywhere.** `stage_overflow` is 0 in all seven runs, on both
arms, at both placements, in every chunk. The base never reads a pooled expert in place on this
lane: at the phase's end the coldest routed layer still held 344 of its 512 units, so the most any
layer pools is 168 units = 173 MiB, against a ring half of 300.14 MiB. The ring is sized as
`f x layer_max` with `layer_max` the *largest* routed layer (937 MiB) while a layer only ever pools
its non-resident share (527 MiB x 35 % = 173 MiB here), so `f x layer_max` over-provisions it by
the ratio of the largest layer to the pooled part of a typical one -- 1.7x at its tightest, ~3.7x on
average. In the audit's sweep `ring/2` was 261 MiB against a 257-unit (265 MiB) pooled layer, i.e.
just over; here it is not, in either placement.

**Prefill, inside the noise.** medians of three base runs and two change runs: 3053 vs 3112 tok/s
(+1.9 %), prefill step seconds 91.2 vs 90.2 (-1.1 %), median TTFT 76.3 vs 73.8 s (-3.3 %); the
pairs disagree in sign (+4.9 %, -5.7 %), and the second placement gives -0.7 %. The same binary
alone moves 4.9 % between two back-to-back runs (and decode 76 %), because the host was at load
average 84-92 on 48 threads. Nothing reaches the spread.

**What the change cost, unchanged.** The 1 GiB ring is paid out of the experts: at the same
`already held` the two 16-session runs differ by ring only, experts 10.83 -> 10.70 GiB. Staged units
and copies move by more between two runs of the same binary than between the arms (base 629550 ->
802714 units, -27 %, same binary), so they say nothing.

## Why it does not merge

The merge bar wants the targeted op measurably faster. The targeted traffic -- pooled experts read
in place -- is zero on this lane in both placements, so there is nothing for the stager to gain and
the measured difference is the machine's. What the change does where such traffic exists is
plausible (move it ahead, and make a heavy layer's leftover go out at its own first op rather than
synchronously), but plausible is not measured, and the ring's cost is real: at 8 sessions it takes
424 MiB of the expert share (576 -> 1024 MiB ring, 0.42 GiB less residency), and in the 16-session
placement 130 MiB (884 -> 1024 MiB), where the experts' share is already the scarce one. Merging
a lossy lever on a hope is exactly what CARDS.md §2 rules out, so the item closes without code.

The audit's own arithmetic still describes a real hole -- a layer whose pooled part exceeds `ring/2`
is streamed synchronously, and at 8 x 60K with a 261 MiB half that was 819-950 units a chunk. The
gap here is that the *placement* decides whether the hole is open at all, and this lane's does not
open it.

## What a re-proposal would need

- A workload that actually overflows. The condition is `max_l pooled_l > f x layer_max / 2`, i.e. a
  placement where a layer's pooled part is more than half of `f` of the largest layer. On this box
  that needs a smaller `ring/2` than 168 MiB, or a colder layer than 344 residents: more KV held
  (`max-num-seqs` 16 only got to 41 % expert residency and still 0), a longer heat history so the
  per-layer spread grows beyond sd 75, or a layer size closer to the largest one. The audit's sweep
  had it; a single longctx phase here does not.
- Then the verify metric is a real one: `stage_overflow` per chunk before and after, plus prefill
  tok/s, on the same placement.
- If the intent is only "overflow is never read in place", that is worth an item on its own that
  starts from a placement which overflows, and it can then also argue the ring's 424 MiB against the
  copies it saves -- here it could not.
- The two-part staging and the heat order may stand on their own in a placement that overflows; they
  are correct and tested, and they cost nothing at this lane's ring size (the split only fires when a
  layer is above the half). They are not merged because their effect cannot be measured here.
