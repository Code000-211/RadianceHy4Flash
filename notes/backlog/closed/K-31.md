# K-31 (`moe_iqr` at verify heights: multi-tile `TPW` for `nkb >= 8`, `dot_run` up to 3-4 rows): CLOSED -- both arms are slower at every verify height, and at TP4/u4 the kernel already streams its codes at 92-98 % of the card's memory bandwidth

Item as written (backlog K-31, issue #299): lift the `g.nkb < 8u` bar on `TPW` (`libr4d/r4d_moe_iqr.hip:782-798`)
so a wide-K wave takes 2 / 4 weight tiles from `T >= 200 / 480`, as `moe_down_dec` scales `G`; second arm,
`kDotRows` 3-4 so runs of 3-4 rows stay on `dot_run`. Why: in a u3 TP2 c8 DFly2 verify pass (T = 640) routed
gate_up is 47.3 % of the pass and 2.27x `moe_down_dec` against a 1.52x byte ratio, with ~12.5K workgroups a
layer each scanning 512 `off[]` entries. Sibling #296 asks where that gap lives.

## What was tried

Both arms were built at the merge base `0b100d6d` behind test pins and timed, then dropped (not in this PR):
- **Arm 1 (BI):** the decode grid's wide-K `TPW` from a rule, plus a pin (`TPW` 1 / 2 / 4) for tests.
  `moe_iqr_kernel<F, 1, 4, false, 1, TPW>` already exists for the narrow-K band (`TPW` 2), and a `TPW` 4
  instance was added. The selftest compared the pins byte for byte with the rule on every decode and verify shape
  of `moe_gemm_iqr`, Hy4's own included: TP4 768 x 1536 at T 192 / 512 / 752, TP2 1536 x 1536 at T 640. All
  byte-identical.
- **Arm 2 (ORD):** `dot_run` and the body templated on the dot rows `DR` (2, 3 or 4), with a decode-grid pin.
  The selftest checked that every run outside 3..DR rows keeps the rule's bytes, that the moved runs hold the dense
  IQR row's bound, and that each moved run length actually moved. It passed, on new shapes with runs of 1-6 rows.
- `r4d_selftest --case moe_gemm_iqr` with both checks: 219 ok, 0 FAIL (the base: 108 ok, 0 FAIL).

## Where the time goes

**Our setup** (TP4, the u4 IQR-V4 container, DFly2):
- Routed gate_up is `moe_gemm_iqr`: 768 rows (gate and up, 384 each) by K 1536 a rank, so `nkb` = 12, and
  `TPW` is forced to 1 from T = 20.
- A c8 verify step is T = 432..752, mostly 512.
- The whole expert plane is resident, so no pooled reads.
- A served profile at TP4/u4 (`profile-ops`, base `34862f3d`, no MoE kernel change since) gives:

  | | c1 | c8 |
  |---|---|---|
  | gate_up | 63.3 us a call | 237.6 us a call (22.4 % of the step) |
  | down | 35.4 us a call | 118.8 us a call |
  | ratio | 1.79 | 2.00 |

  The u4 byte ratio is about 1.88: gate_up's 590 KB an expert, down's w4nl64 at about 313 KB. So the gap above
  byte scaling is about 6 % here.

**The kernel is bandwidth-bound.** `--perfmoeiqr` (512 experts, top 16, uniform routing, 8 routings in turn) moves
the read experts' codes at:
- 592-625 GB/s at TP4/u4, which is 92-98 % of the R9700's 640 GB/s, with scales and activations on top;
- 508-552 GB/s at the item's TP2/u3, 80-86 %. Three-bit codes cost the same decode ALU a unit for 3/4 of the bytes.

**Fewer blocks lose (arm 1).** Two or four tiles a wave take a half or a quarter of the waves that hide the weight
stream. The run search and its barrier, which they save, are not the cost: no T in 128..768 crosses over.

**The dot path loses past two rows (arm 2).** Its work grows with every row (16 x 4 `sudot4` a unit and row), while
the tile's eight WMMA steps are flat up to 16 rows. Under uniform routing, runs of 3-4 rows are few anyway (the
longest is 4 at T = 512).

**#296's u3/TP2 2.27x** would need gate_up to run 1.5x slower a byte than `moe_down_dec`, and the synthetic u3
gate_up already runs at 80-86 % of peak. What is left lies outside this kernel's grid: the served routing, the
chained form's wait inside the op's time, or the byte ratio itself. It was not measured on a u3 TP2 lane.

## Verdict

Not merged. Arm 1 is byte-identical and 0.4-23 % slower. Arm 2 is inside the dense bound and level to 4 %
slower. Under the merge bar (CARDS.md §2 item 2) neither is faster at any verify height, at our TP4/u4 setup or at
the item's TP2/u3 shape.
