# K-12 (junction warms `fn` during the peer-flag wait): CLOSED — the wait is shorter than the warm, so the warm is 0.63 ms a pass slower, and a latency-tuned warm is exactly neutral

Item as written (backlog K-12, `notes/backlog/optimisation-backlog.md`): "`r4d_ar_ihc_junction.hip:137-200` (`:178` spin
on the peer flag while 511 threads sit at `__syncthreads`), arithmetic `r4d_ihc.h:207-260`. Let the idle threads touch
`fn` (8 x 12288 f32 = 393 KB), the two gain vectors and `h` (24 KB) during the wait." Why: "junction 15.3 us x 84 =
1.28 ms = 9.3 % of a c1 pass ... est. -2..-4 us each = 0.17-0.34 ms". BI (loads only), effort S. Its two references are
the reverted tree's: `:178` is the spin, and `r4d_ihc.h:207-260` is `junction_row`.

This branch built it twice — as written, and again with the read latency tuned — measured both on one lane, and reverts
them: as written the junction is **0.63 ms a pass slower** and the served step loses 0.57 ms (+4.2 %); tuned it is
**neutral** (junction 2.865 against 2.859 ms a pass, step 13.711 against 13.725 ms, both inside the noise of the lane).
The output is byte-identical either way, so the change is BI as claimed. It is not a gain: the 511 threads leave the
wait only when their last read lands, the warm outlasts the spin it was meant to hide behind, and the spin cannot be
longer than the 442 kB the warm has to read through one block.

## What the code says (at `761a0ff7`, after this branch's revert)

This branch's head is `761a0ff7`, and at it `libr4d/r4d_ar_ihc_junction.hip` and `libr4d/r4d_ihc.h` are `afb77ae4`'s
byte for byte (the revert `971af786` is the whole of the difference) — so these are the before arm's lines too.

- The wait is thread 0's alone and it is a wait for the *peer*: `r4d_ar_ihc_junction.hip:174-179` is thread 0's block,
  and `:176` stores its own release on the peer's flags (`arij_store_rel(&peer_flags[bk], sq)`) before `:178-179` spin
  on its half (`arij_load_acq(&my_flags[bk])`) until the peer's rows are in. The other 511 threads pass the
  `__syncthreads()` in front of it (`:173`), fall through the `if` and stop at the one behind it (`:181`), so they sit
  there for the whole of the spin. That is the idle window the item points at, and it is real. (The barrier at `:181`
  is where they wait, not one further on: past it every thread enters `r4d_archk_wait` (`:183-188`), whose rehash reads
  the peer's arrived bytes.)
- The arrival is *not* on the far side of the warm: the release store is thread 0's and comes before both the spin and
  the warm, so warming cannot delay the peer. So a launch is `max(spin, warm) + row`: a warm that fits in the spin is
  free, and a warm longer than the spin is added to the launch as a whole.
- What the row reads, in `junction_row` (`r4d_ihc.h:207-260`), whose operand list is `JuncRow` (`:187-203`): `h`
  (`r.hr`, read at `:227` and written back at `:236`; `HC * n` bf16 = 24 KB at HC 4, n 3072), the post norm's gain
  (`r.w_post`, `:214`; `n` f32 = 12 KB), and behind a tail the next norm's gain (`r.w_next`, `:249`; 12 KB) and `fn`
  (`acc_piece` at `:237`; `R = POST ? 2 * HC : HC` rows of `HC * n` f32 = 48 KB a row: 8 rows, the item's 393 KB,
  where POST = 1, and 4 rows, 192 KiB, in the one POST = 0 shape that has a tail). So a POST = 1, TAIL = 1 launch warms
  442,368 B (432 KiB), the one POST = 0, TAIL = 1 launch 240 KiB (245,760 B), and a TAIL = 0 launch 36 KiB — `h` and
  the post gain only.
- The junction is 84 launches a pass at TP2 here: five `ar_ihc_junction_2rank_kernel` instantiations, 42 + 37 + 3 + 1 + 1
  a pass. `warm_row` gates the next gain and the `fn` reads on TAIL, not on POST (which only sets how many `fn` rows it
  warms), so 80 of the 84 warm `fn`: the 42 + 37 POST = 1 launches at 442,368 B and the single `<4,0,1,1,0>` at 240 KiB,
  35.3 MB (33.7 MiB) a pass. The four that warm none of it are the three `<4,0,0,1,0>` and the one `<4,0,0,0,0>`. Every
  byte of that is a byte the row reads again immediately, so the warm adds no traffic, only time in front of the row.

## What was built and measured, then reverted

Both variants live in `libr4d/r4d_ihc.h` and `libr4d/r4d_ar_ihc_junction.hip` and are not kept:

- `r4d_ihc::warm_row<HC, POST, TAIL>(row, tid)`: thread `t > 0` reads word `t - 1, t - 1 + 511, ...` of each of the
  row's read ranges in 16-byte `uint4` loads (the granularity `acc_piece`, `norm_row` and the row's own `h` read at),
  XORs them into a register and hands it to an empty `asm volatile`, so neither the compiler nor anyone else can drop
  the reads. Loads only — no store, no value read out, thread 0 not taking part.
- Called from the kernel's `if (tid == 0) { spin } else { warm_row }` — in the warm's own commit `c9dfc357`,
  `r4d_ar_ihc_junction.hip:180-187`, the call `r4d_ihc::warm_row<HC, POST, TAIL>(w, (uint32_t)tid)` at `:186`. Nothing of
  it is in the tree now (`971af786` reverts it), and `warm_row` itself was `c9dfc357`'s `r4d_ihc.h:217-238`, under the
  comment at `:206-216`.
- Variant A: one accumulator, one load a step — what the item describes.
- Variant B: four accumulators, four loads a step (four independent XOR chains), the same ranges and the same 16-byte
  words. Same reads, ~4x the reads in flight a warp; a chain of dependent reads is what would outlast a short wait,
  and this is the cheapest way to test whether that is the whole of the problem. It is 4 registers and 4 lines of index
  arithmetic more, nothing else.

**Correctness.** `tests/r4d_emu/test_ihc.cpp` (a new case set, 22 cases; the file is the item's Verify step): the warm's
own accounting — every operand's last page reached and no page beyond it, the tail on and off, the `h` row group's own
offset, thread 0 reading nothing, and a page taken away from any one operand faults the warm — plus the junction's own
bytes unchanged against the emulated peer. The checks are byte-exact (the warm reads and never writes, so any difference
in a kernel's output is a failure and there is no tolerance to pick). Red without the change: `fn` warmed to
`HC * n / 8` words instead of `/ 4` fails it (`the warm stopped short of fn's last page`), and the file does not compile
against the tree without the change at all (`warm_row` absent). `ctest -R '^r4d_emu'` 24/24, `r4d_emu_ihc` 22 cases 0
failures, and the same for variant B.

`r4d_selftest --case ar_rmsnorm_ihc_junction` and `--case ar_rmsnorm_ihc_junction_host`, two cards (`gpu2` on iks): ok
on every check of both cases, in the before arm and with the warm (a loads-only warm cannot change an output, and does
not; run on the before arm too so the two arms' selftests are the same test). The `greedy` phase's four requests are
byte-identical before and with the warm (4/4 texts, one of them the 5.7K arrival): the item's TP2 greedy bit-equal.

**The RDNA4 ISA suite** (`bash .radisa/check-tree.sh --compile-commands build/compile_commands.json --changed-since
afb77ae4 --jobs 16`) on the change: `r4d_ar_ihc_junction.hip`, PASS, 0 errors, no wait-counter or barrier finding on
the junction instantiations. Reverted with the code.

**Variant A, as the item describes it: +0.63 ms a pass, +22 % of the junction.** The two POST = 1 instantiations are the
ones that pay, as the byte counts above say they must: +10.96 us and +4.15 us a launch over 42 + 37 launches, 0.61 ms of
the 0.63. The other way, the 4 launches that warm nothing of `fn` (TAIL = 0, 36 KiB, and that little does fit the spin)
are 3.6-3.8 us a launch *faster* (14.41 -> 10.63 and 18.87 -> 15.31 us) and the one `<4,0,1,1,0>` (240 KiB: `h`, both
gains, 4 rows of `fn`) 0.4 us faster: -0.015 ms a pass together, well inside the before arm's own 0.07 ms spread of
junction totals (2.902 2.831 2.843). Variant A's four runs spread over only 0.011 ms, so a gap of 0.63 ms is the
change and not the lane.

End to end, the same runs' `c1` phase (`ms_step` from the probe's own report) and what it comes to at one stream:

| arm | ms a step | tok/s |
|:--|:--|--:|
| before (b3, b4) | 13.7203, 13.7286 | 72.9 |
| A, one chain (a1 … a4) | 14.2872, 14.2865, 14.3010, 14.3072 | 69.9 |
| B, four chains (c1, c2) | 13.6972, 13.7255 | 73.0 |

Variant A's step is +0.57 ms (+4.2 %): the junction's loss minus the 0.015 ms the small warms give back.
Variant B's is -0.01 ms against the before arm — the same step time, in the other direction of the lane's ±0.03 ms noise.

**Variant B says the dependency chain was most of the cost, and that recovering it buys nothing.** Four reads in flight
take the `<4,1,1,1,0>` instantiation 3.8 us *below* the before arm (21.20 against 24.96) and the bulk one to 3.1 us
above it (55.92 against 52.80): the two cancel to 2.865 against 2.859 ms a pass, which is the noise of a 2.86 ms
number on this lane. So the lever has one of two states — a warm longer than the wait, which costs its excess; or a warm
short enough to fit, which saves only the row's own streaming *and* only up to the wait. The measurement puts the second
outcome at 0.00 ms: the row's own reads already arrive as fast as one block can take them, so asking for the same
bytes from the same block a few microseconds earlier moves nothing, and the wait it asks during is short (the before arm's own 52.8 us launch is 42
launches of row work, not a long wait: `max(spin, warm) = warm` already in variant A means `spin < 11 us`).

That is the item's premise measured: it assumes `spin + 2..4 us > warm` for 442 kB through one block, and the two
quantities are the wrong way round on this card and shape. It also means a third variant with the same reads cannot
get there — the reads are the floor, and they are already at the point where removing the dependency chain removes
their whole cost and still leaves 0.00 ms to gain.

## What a later card would need

- The window, not the reads: measure the spin itself before trusting it (a `s_memrealtime` pair around thread 0's loop
  into a scratch record would do it; the AQL stamps already separate the launch's dispatch time from the pass's, which
  is how far this card got without it). A wait of a few us cannot hide 442 kB; a card that finds a *long* wait (a
  bigger TP, a different all-reduce shape) can revisit this change with the numbers above as its lower bound.
- Or a smaller thing to hide: if the row's inter-rank `h` half (the part that comes over the wire, not `fn`) were what
  the idle threads fetched ahead, the bytes would be 24 KB, not 442 kB — but the item explicitly says "not the closed
  'faster wire': the wire is untouched", so that is another card's question, not this one's.

## What K-13 inherits

K-13 ("split the plain part's piece sums over several blocks", dep "K-12 first, then measure") does not depend on this
result: it changes who owns the waves, not what happens during the wait, and its "+1 % on top of K-12" was a guess
about a K-12 that does not exist. Its own measurement stands on its own feet, and the dependency it records can be
dropped.

## Seen and left alone

- **The trunk did not compile at this branch's base.** `2c9f7e1c` (the #190 resolution) left two functions unclosed:
  `arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash.cpp` (`head_reads_all_rows`) and `tests/hyv4flash_arch_test.cpp` (the P-08 test, before the
  K-49 one). This branch carries the two braces back as its own commit so that anything can build and be measured;
  `moebius/card-20` has the same fix for the first file, and the commit can be dropped on a rebase that takes it from
  there. It is not K-12's code.
- **The small instantiations got *faster* in variant A** (14.44/13.88/14.92 → 11.24/10.80/10.72/9.76 us) while the
  `fn` ones got slower, and in variant B they are 11.84. Those are the TAIL = 0 shapes, a 36 KiB warm against 442,368 B:
  that little does fit the spin, so the row then finds `h` and its gain in L2. Three launches a pass (each a different row
  group), 3.6-3.8 us each, ~0.011 ms of a pass: not worth chasing on its own, and it is the model's other side — a warm
  shorter than the wait is free, and gives the row's own reads back.
- **The local lane could not be used.** Its card pair was held for the whole of this card by another run's
  `place_test` (CARDS §1's "use the other lane" applies), and in this container a radiance launched outside the
  scheduler falls back to the host backend, so iks-A is not only the free pair but the only one that could measure.
  Its absolute numbers are its own (junction 52.8 / 25.0 us a launch against the local baseline's 12.6-15.3); what this
  card reads is the ratio between two builds on one lane.
- **Before arm run b2 and the variant's stamps files are gone**: the lane's tree on iks is unpacked fresh for every call
  and its `runs/` is not shipped, so anything not copied back before the next call is lost. b1, b3 and b4 are the same
  arm and agree to 0.1 us, and variant B's two runs are reported per run in the step table, so nothing that the
  conclusion rests on is missing — but a lane helper that keeps `runs/` across calls would have saved two of them.
