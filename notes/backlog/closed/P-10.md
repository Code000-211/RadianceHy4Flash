# P-10 (indexer score plane in row tiles): CLOSED — the 118 MB come back, but the two ops the tiling re-issues cost more than the traffic they save

Item as written (backlog P-10, `notes/backlog/optimisation-backlog.md`): "Indexer score plane
`[max_tok, max_ctx/4]` f32: row tiles. Area plugin · BI · Gain arena peak -134 MB at 64K context (-268 MB at
128K): more room for the ring / experts during prefill · Conf medium · Effort M · Deps K-43 (same kernels need a
row offset). Change: `hyv4flash_indexer.h:63, 81, 390, 403-409`: score + select per 256-1024 rows."
Audits: `audit/C-plugin-graph.md` F17 (`idx_score` / `qsa_select` / the plane grow with context, "gain small in
time"), the same terms §5 Z-26.

This branch built the item in full — five commits, `dffc7083` (the tiling) through `c6500430` (its tests) —
passed every card-less check, and then measured it on the `iks-B` lane. The arena gain is real and larger than
the item claimed, the output is byte-identical and the end to end does not regress, but **the ops the item
targets are measurably slower**: `idx_score` +24 ms and `qsa_select` +20 ms of device time over the same two
prefills, +57 ms a run with the rebase and offset ops. CARDS.md §2 item 2 asks for the targeted op to be
"measurably faster in ms", so per the card ("Otherwise write `closed/P-10.md` with the numbers and the reason,
and close the card without merging code") this run reverts the five commits and leaves this note.

## What the branch built, and what it passed

`arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash_indexer.h` scored and selected a prefill chunk as one launch and now does it in row
tiles of `kScoreTile = 256` rows: `score_rows = taps_on() ? T : min(T, kScoreTile)`, the wire's `max_tiles` (the
ceil of `max_tok / score_rows`), per-tile `brow_slice` views of `qcodes` / `qscale` / `iwf`, a rebased `cu` in place of
the chunk's (`op_rebase`), the P-09 snapshot planes written at `sel_out->first + t0`, and one
`op_tile_off` (`iota`) a layer per step. `tests/hyv4flash_arch_test.cpp` grew the packed (`dsa_score`), bf16
(`idx_score`) and P-09 snapshot cases, `tests/r4d_emu/` the emulator's tile check for the packed score.

Both arms are the full build of their commit on that machine. A prefill chunk is 1984 rows, so every chunk of
`pf6` / `pf20` is taller than one tile: before the change a sparse layer scored the chunk in one launch and
selected once, after it 8 tiles of 256 rows each. The two arms are two different plugins — the plan declares
2831 ops / 167 buffers before and 2855 / 169 after (the two new buffers are the tile offsets and the rebased
`cu`).

### Output parity

`greedy` (temperature 0, 256 tokens on three short prompts and 64 on a 22,000-character one) gave the same four
texts in all six runs of either kind, byte for byte: `d6da4fb3`, `0699709c`, `e7a23c5d`, `466e53a2`

| | before | after | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| 64K: activation arena, plan | 1.40 GiB | 1.29 GiB | **−0.11 GiB = −118 MB** |
| 64K: activation arena, levels | 1.35 GiB | 1.25 GiB | −0.10 GiB |
| 64K: the 1024-token level | 694.02 MiB | 646.02 MiB | −48.00 MiB |
| 64K: claimable | 29.68 GiB | 29.79 GiB | +0.11 GiB |
| 64K: kv cache | 4.29 GiB | 4.40 GiB | +0.11 GiB |
| 128K: activation arena, plan | 1.52 GiB | 1.30 GiB | **−0.22 GiB = −236 MB** |
| 128K: activation arena, levels | 1.48 GiB | 1.26 GiB | −0.22 GiB |
| 128K: claimable | 29.55 GiB | 29.77 GiB | +0.22 GiB |

The 128K rows are one startup of each arm (`nospec128k.yaml`, phase `c1`): what they read is the plan, and the
plan does not depend on the load.

The arithmetic agrees with the change: the plane is `[rows, blocks]` f32 with `blocks = max_ctx/4 + 1`, so at
64K it was `2048 × 16385 × 4 B = 134.2 MB` and is `256 × 16385 × 4 B = 16.8 MB` — 117.4 MB freed, the −0.11 GiB
measured; at 128K the same step is 234.9 MB, the −0.22 GiB measured. The item's −134 / −268 MB counted the
plane's whole old size as freed, and the one tile that stays costs 16.8 MB (64K) / 33.6 MB (128K).

One plane's worth, not twelve: the plan holds 167 buffers for 42 layers, so the twelve sparse layers' score
buffers — the same shape, transient — share one slot, and that slot is what shrinks. What the arena gives back
goes to the elastic pool and lands in the KV cache (4.29 to 4.40 GiB a rank): the item's "more room for the
ring / experts during prefill".

### The two ops the tiling re-issues (`profile-ops: true`, the same phases in both arms)

12 sparse layers, the table's last dump of each run (run total), device event pairs — the kernel's own time,
`wait us` excluded. Three runs an arm; the median is quoted.

| op | before, calls | before, total ms | before, ns a row | after, calls | after, total ms | after, ns a row |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `idx_score` | 213 | 93.5 / **100.0** / 101.4 | 236 | 1302 | 123.4 / **123.9** / 124.4 | 371 |
| `qsa_select` | 213 | 28.2 / **29.7** / 33.5 | 70 | 1302 | 49.4 / **50.2** / 49.7 | 150 |
| `sub_i32` | 213 | 2.5 / **1.7** / 1.6 | — | 1302 | 12.5 / **13.0** / 14.1 | — |
| `iota` | 0 | 0 / **0** / 0 | — | 12 | 1.7 / **1.7** / 1.8 | — |

213 calls across the twelve layers before the change, 1302 after: the same prefill steps, each 1984-row chunk
now 8 tiles of 256 rows (a prompt's tail chunk is shorter and takes fewer, and the prefix cache had shortened
the second prompt). The useful work is unchanged; the per-row rate is not: `idx_score` pays 57 % more a row and
`qsa_select` 114 %, which is the per-launch floor (the block table, the live counts, `cu`) paid once a tile
instead of once a chunk. The tiling does buy locality — a tile's plane is 16.8 MB instead of 134 MB — but that
traffic was not what the op spent its time on: over these two prefills the tiling costs **+23.9 ms `idx_score`,
+20.5 ms `qsa_select`, +10.8 ms `sub_i32`, +1.7 ms `iota` = +56.9 ms**, and saves none.

The `--match 'idx_score\|qsa_select'` table of `agg.py` cannot show this: the AQL stamps record only the
chained decode tape, and in these runs the csv's last row (`1371359125971101`) ends 6 ms before the
`pf6` window opens (`1371359132081823`) — every stamped pass is a `greedy` decode pass, where a step is one row
and the tiling is a no-op. `profile-ops` is what covers prefill, and the server flags that run as "not
comparable with an unprofiled one"; the e2e below is from the unprofiled runs.

### End to end (unprofiled, `nospec.yaml`, the same prompts in both arms)

`pf6` is `probe.py`'s 22,000-character prompt (the backlog's 6.7K prompt), `pf20` its 72,000-character one
(25,414 prompt tokens, as the probe reports); 8 tiles a sparse layer a chunk after, 1 launch before.

| run | pf6 TTFT (s) | pf20 TTFT (s) |
|---|---|---|
| before 1 / 2 / 3 | 2.240 / 2.353 / 2.368 | 7.378 / 7.464 / 7.496 |
| after 1 / 2 / 3 | 2.184 / 2.214 / 2.253 | 7.392 / 7.367 / 7.436 |
| median, before → after | 2.353 → **2.214** (−5.9 %) | 7.464 → **7.392** (−1.0 %) |

Not worse, and the direction is the arena: with 0.11 GiB more KV the prefix cache holds more, and the same
prompts found more of themselves cached (hit 0.896 → 0.901, expert misses a step 8462 / 26858 → 8119 / 26112).
Decode throughput is not quoted: other jobs shared the machine and the decode step moved between 7.0 and
12.1 ms across my own runs.

## Why it is closed, and what would reopen it

- CARDS.md §2 item 2 fails, and fails on the sign: the item's two ops are 24 % and 69 % slower over the same
  work. The e2e is a little better (item 3 holds) and the output is byte-identical (item 1 holds), so the trade
  is +118 MB of arena and −5.9 % / −1.0 % of TTFT against +0.8 % of a 7.4 s prefill (56.9 ms instrumented;
  about 17 ms from a launch at 8 µs over the 1089 extra calls an op makes, both prefills and all twelve
  layers, unprofiled). That is a **memory** item
  whose cost is time, which is what the item's own audit said ("gain small in time") and what the bar does not
  accept.
- Nothing about the measurement is lost by closing: the numbers are above, so a re-proposal does not need a
  re-run.
- What would make it merge: a bar that reads "the arena peak falls, the e2e is not worse, the ops no worse than
  X %" — a policy decision for the owner, not for a card; or an engine that issues a chunk's tiles as **one**
  launch (a row-tile count on the op, the kernel looping internally), which keeps the 118 MB and pays the
  per-launch floor once; that is a different change, in the engine's op forms, and it is not this item's.
