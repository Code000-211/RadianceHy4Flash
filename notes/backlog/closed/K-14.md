# K-14 (`merge_sink` at 32 heads): CLOSED — the body is 1.1 us at the item's geometry; the 19 us bracket is not its work

Item as written (backlog K-14, `notes/backlog/optimisation-backlog.md`): "`r4d_mla_sink.hip:443-482`: (a) value loads
wait on the denominator load (`:452-455`): load `part[s]` unconditionally (the fold already skips den <= 0, `:461`), load
`sinks[head]` early, store 4 bf16 as one 8-byte store (`:478-481`); (b) check the (M, heads) x 128 grid at 32 heads x 8
slots for per-head serial slot folds with dependent L2 round trips and for the stable-split scratch layout (16
workgroups write, 32 read)."
Why: "M: `merge_sink` 42 x 15.2 us = 0.64 ms = 4.7 % of a c1 pass; sliding layers 19.0 us, sparse 6.3 us with the same
key count (short context). Arithmetic is ~0.5 us. Exclusive attribution inside a chained trio is fuzzy, so first kbench
the row alone (heads 32, slots 8, M 1, chained and plain)."
Verify: "kbench `mla_attn_paged_sink` M = 1 cases (93 in `kernels_hy4.rkb`); `tests/r4d_emu/test_mla_sink.cpp` bytes;
stamps." BI, effort S (measure) + S-M.

This branch built (a), checked (b) and measured the row alone, plain and chained, on one card at the item's geometry. It
reverts (a): the merge's own work is **1.1 us** at 32 heads x 8 slots and **1.9 us** at the 20-slot law, the change
moves the op by 0.04-0.10 us (0.04 us — inside the row's own +-0.5 % — at the item's row), and (b) finds nothing to
remove. The anomaly is not in the merge's body: the op that contains it — `mla_sink_rows` and this merge, the expand is
a launch of its own — is 8.83 us at that geometry and the merge is 1.15 of it, so a 19 us merge cannot be the kernel's
work at that geometry.

## What the code says (at HEAD, where this branch leaves it)

- `merge_sink_body` (`libr4d/r4d_mla_sink.hip:442-486`): one workgroup a (row, head), thread `t` holding columns
  `4t..4t+3`. Slot `s`'s maximum and denominator (`src[s * PART + D]`, `+ D + 1`) and, when `part_den[s] > 0`, its
  `float4` partial are loaded in one unrolled pass, then the slots with a visible key are folded in ascending order
  (`sink_fold`, the fused form's rule) with the values in registers, and `sinks[head]` is read once at the end
  (`sink_inv`). The four bf16 leave as four 2-byte stores at `out + r * m.orr + head * m.oh + 4 * t`.
- An empty slot (`m.splits > 1 && begin >= end`) writes only `dst[D] = -INFINITY; dst[D + 1] = 0;`; every slot owns its
  own `PART` floats (2064 B), so a slot's value region is in bounds whether or not the sink ran on it.
- The grid is `dim3(rows, heads)` x 128 threads, so at 32 heads and one row it is 32 blocks; the scratch is one
  contiguous `splits * PART` block per (row, head) (`scratch + ((r * heads + head) * m.splits + split) * PART`).
- `chain = r4d_geti_or(a, "chain", 0) != 0 && rows <= 64` chooses the chained wrapper (`r4d_chain.h`: no AQL barrier,
  the merge spins on the chain's earlier launches); kbench launches the plain form unless `--chain-forms` is passed.

## What was built and measured, then reverted

The three parts of (a), lossless by construction:

- `const float sink = sinks[(long)head * m.ss];` hoisted above the slot loop, so the last thing the body waits on is not
  a load issued after the fold.
- `part[s] = *(const float4*)(src + s * PART + 4 * t);` unconditional (still under `s < m.splits`): the fold skips
  `den <= 0` anyway, and gating a load on a value loaded just before it costs a round trip rather than saving one. The
  empty slot's values are read and never folded, so no bit moves (the emu suite's fused-form and `stable 1` comparisons
  are byte-equal, below).
- The four bf16 as one 8-byte store where the out tensor's strides leave a thread's columns 8-byte aligned
  (`if (!(o0 & 3))`, `o0 = r * orr + head * oh + 4 * t`; `4t` is a multiple of 4, so the test is uniform over the
  block), and the four 2-byte stores where they do not, since `row_tensor_ok` only asks `stride[1] >= width` and an odd
  stride is legal.

The out-stride path got an emu case as well (`launch(..., int out_pad)`: head stride `L + pad`, row stride
`heads * (L + pad) + pad`, pad 1 and 2, which between them put both stores on the wire, and the padding checked to stay
the sentinel). It is reverted with the rest; as written it also pushed `r4d_emu_mla_sink` past its 120 s ctest timeout,
so a retry should pick a smaller geometry or a `--quick` case.

**What the numbers say.**

- The merge's own work at the item's geometry is **1.15 us**; 8 slots x 516 floats x 32 heads = 528 KiB read at
  ~460 GB/s, which a range the sink kernel has just written can give, and the item's own arithmetic is ~0.5 us of that.
  19 us at that rate would be 8.7 MiB — 17 times the entire scratch of the launch.
- The op measured here is `mla_sink_rows` + `merge_sink` only (`libr4d/r4d_mla_sink.hip:677-682`); the expand is a
  separate op (`arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash_attn.h:414`, issued after the paged op at `:712-717`), so this 8.83 us is not
  the served trio's total and this note does not use it as one. The merge is 13 % of the op and the other 87 % is
  `mla_sink_rows` (the cut arm, 7.72 us, is the sink plus an empty merge launch), so nothing a merge does can turn 42
  launches into 0.64 ms.
- The served trio is wider than that, and its brackets sum to more than the two launches measured alone: 7.3 + 19.0 +
  4.4 = 30.7 us in a sliding layer and 11.3 + 6.3 + 9.4 = 27.0 in a sparse one, against 8.8-9.6 us for the sink and the
  merge on one card. The brackets therefore carry 8-18 us the kernels' content does not explain; kbench's chained form
  cannot bound that (it skips the wait when no engine record follows), so this note leaves its origin open.
- What the 19.0 us bracket is not is the merge's slot chain, which is what (a) targets. In kbench the body does grow with
  the slots (1.15 us at 8, 1.88 at 20) and stays at 1.09-1.19 us from 16 to 64 heads at 8 slots; in the served layers it goes the other
  way, the 16-head 20-slot merge bracketed at 6.3 us against the 32-head 8-slot one's 19.0, so the 19.0 is not the
  per-thread slot round trips.
- (b) finds no serialization to remove: the slot fold is register-resident with all slot loads issued up front (one
  `float4` and two halves of a line a slot, coalesced across the 128 threads), the scratch is contiguous per (row,
  head) so the readers walk 16.5 KiB runs, and the 16 writers / 32 readers traffic is 528 KiB, which the row measured
  alone reads at ~460 GB/s.

A first version of this note read the 8.83 us as the served trio's total and counted the expand in it; that was wrong —
the expand is its own launch — and the bullets above are the accounting that replaces it. The close rests on the merge's
own body and on (b), not on the op total being the trio's.

## Why it closes without code

The card's merge bar asks the targeted op to be measurably faster. It is not: the merge's own body goes 1.15 -> 1.11 us
and the op 8.87 -> 8.83 us at the item's row, against that row's own +-0.5 % (0.04 us) and a 0.64 ms claim — two orders
of magnitude short. The only rows that move outside their spread are the 20-slot ones (9.66 -> 9.56 us, +-0.7 %), where
the unconditional value load saves ~0.1 us of a 1.9 us body; that is 3-5 % of a kernel whose 42 launches are 0.6 % of a
c1 pass (80 us of 13.6 ms), i.e. below the item's own "0" for c8 and inside the noise of anything that could check it
end to end.

The larger result is that the item's premise does not hold: the merge's body does not cost 19 us at 32 heads — it is
1.15 us at the item's geometry and 1.9 at the 20-slot law — so the 0.64 ms (4.7 % of a c1 pass) is not a merge-body
prize, and neither (a) nor (b) can move what the 19.0 us bracket is made of. K-15 ("fold the merge into the expand",
+0.5-1.5 %) and K-17's launch count inherit an open question rather than the measured prize their Deps line assumes:
what the chained brackets carry, and whether one launch fewer removes it. If this is retried, start from the served
trio's brackets (served lane, stamps), not from the merge's inner loop.
