## 3. Why the premise does not hold here

The item's premise is that the three engram op groups are traffic-bound on the traffic it removes.
On this lane none of them is.

- **`engram_gate`** (the item's `250-301`, weights re-read per row). h is `[2048, 4, 1536]` bf16 =
  25 MB; the kernel reads it three times (three sums of squares and a dot) and writes it once, so
  ~100 MB of its 551 us = **181 GB/s, 12 % of the card's VRAM bandwidth**: it is bound by the three
  dependent passes and their wave reductions, not by traffic. The weights it re-reads are four
  vectors of `n = 3072` = **24 KB a layer**, read by that layer's 512 concurrent blocks: they stay in
  L2, so reading them once for four rows instead of once a row saves L2 hits, not DRAM. That is the
  measured -1.5 %, and the four-row grouping also has to pay 50 SGPR more (the lint below).
- **`embed_lookup_q`** (the item's `74-78`, the gather into a host-domain plane). 2048 rows x 16
  heads of 96 B random reads in the 14.75 GiB pinned fp8 table, plus 6.0 MB of bf16 rows written:
  750 us is **12 GB/s effective**, a latency-bound gather, not a streaming one. The plane's domain
  moves 6.0 MB of writes, and the norm's read-back, off and on PCIe: at ~25 GB/s that is ~0.25 ms of
  traffic *overlapped with the table's random reads*, and the measurement puts it at -0.4 %. The
  three ordered launches the item counts go with it: ~8 us a layer x 4 layers = 32 us of a 472 ms
  chunk = 0.007 %.
- **`engram_norm`** (the item's `188-197`, the row read twice). The second read is 3 KiB a (row,
  head), one row, immediately after the first: L1/L2 hot either way. 43.6 us a call in both arms.
- The audit's M figures do not reproduce on this machine: the engram is 5.44 ms a chunk (1.15 %), not
  10.2 ms (1.8-2.4 %), and per call the gather is 754 us against M's 1040, the gate 552 against 311,
  the norm 43.6 against 412. Whatever the norm's 412 us was, it is not this path.

## 5. What a retry would have to change

The lever is not traffic. If the engram is to get faster at prefill on this machine, the measurement
points at:

- `engram_gate`: three dependent passes over `h` with a wave reduction between them. One pass that
  keeps the row in registers (like the norm now does, `hc * n * 2 = 12 KiB` a row a block for 8 rows:
  too much for LDS at 2048 rows, so a wave- or tile-shaped block that stages its own fragment) would
  remove two of the three `h` reads and two barriers; that is worth up to ~2/3 of 2.2 ms if the
  pass count is what binds it, and it can be checked with `profile-ops` on the same lane in one
  afternoon.
- `embed_lookup_q`: latency-bound on 32K random 96 B reads in a 14.75 GiB pin. Nothing about the
  destination plane helps; a deeper in-flight window (more rows a wave, or `__ldg`-style gather with
  more outstanding loads a lane) does. The gate's own grouping machinery is already there for it.
- Nothing else in the item is above noise. The audit's M numbers should be re-taken on this host
  before the next engram item is chosen: its three op times differ by 2-10x from here.
