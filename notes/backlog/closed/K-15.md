# K-15 — `merge_sink` folded into the expand: closed without code

The item (notes/backlog/optimisation-backlog.md, K-15, as it was opened):

> #### K-15 Fold `merge_sink` into the expand (G-3h alternative) or `mla_sink_rows` + merge one launch
> - Area kernel+plugin · BI · Gain 0.5-1.5 % / 0.5 % / 1 % / 0 · Conf medium · Effort M · Deps K-14 result
> - Change: when the sink is split, the expand workgroup (a head, 16 tokens) merges its head's
>   partials into LDS (16 KB) with `merge_sink_body`'s fold, then multiplies (`r4d_mla_sink.hip:443-482, 673-678`;
>   `r4d_mla_kv_bf16.hip:262-299`; `hyv4flash_attn.h:665-676`).
> - Why: -1 launch and the `lat` round trip per layer.
> - Verify: fusion oracle; emu bytes.

**Closed, without code, on the served measurement**: the fusion was built and measured on one lane,
and it does not save the step it is used in. Served c1 (the phase the item's 0.5-1.5 % names) goes
70.344 / 72.483 tok/s at `165daed7` to 68.503 / 71.529 tok/s on the branch, while the same branch
binary with the fold switched off serves 70.030 / 72.215 — the fold costs 1.4-2.6 % of the decode
step rather than gaining anything, and the device is not where it goes: per pass the kernels are
busy 13287 -> 13305 us (+0.14 %) while the wall grows 13815 -> 14191 us (+2.7 %). The cause is
visible in the same stamps: the fused row has no chained form, so the three chained launches a layer
become two unchained ones, and the chained forms' overlap is worth more than the launch the item
removes (the pass goes from 1270 chained + 47 plain launches to 1144 chained + 131 plain). The
fold's own work is not the whole of it either: at c8, where the sink is split 8 ways and the row
count is high, the group is 146 us a pass cheaper, while at c1 it costs 88 us more and the step
pays that too.

The branch is reverted at this note's sibling commit: `git revert 165daed7..ae420a53`, so the tree
is `165daed7`'s again apart from these notes (`git diff 165daed7 -- . ':(exclude)notes'` is empty).

## The stamps (c1, one pass, 42 layers)

| dispatches a pass | 1317 (1270 chained + 47 plain) | 1275 (1144 chained + 131 plain) |
| `mla_sink_rows` | 42 x 15.5 = 652 us | 42 x 11.4 = 478 us |
| `merge_sink` | 42 x 15.5 = 651 us | — |

| device busy a pass | 13287 us | 13305 us (+18) |
| wall a pass | 13815 us | 14191 us (+376) |

The "exclusive" figure is `scripts/backlog/agg.py`'s: a launch's own time from its start, or from
the end of the launch in front of it when it was submitted before that end (a chained form spins,
its neighbour does not). That makes a chained kernel's figure "the work it adds after its
predecessor", which is the number to compare with an unchained one, but it also means the base's
three figures do not add up to one elapsed time: their brackets overlap (the raw durations of a
chained c1 pass sum to 3926 us and cover 1691 us). The union of the group's kernels is not a
comparable figure either: the base's first kernel spins for a launch outside the group. So the
like-for-like figures are the per-kernel ones above, the c8 split, wall, device-busy, and the
served step.

At c8 (42 layers; 30 fused and 12 pairs on the branch, the sparse layers' 8 rows being over the
bound of four): base `mla_sink_rows` 625 + `merge_sink` 1169 + `kv_expand_gate_quant` 204 = 1998 us;
branch `mla_sink_rows` 607 + 12 pairs' merge 105 + 30 fused 1067 + 12 expands 73 = 1852 us (-146).

kbench on the branch tree (`data/kernels_hy4.rkb`, 214 cases timed), the fused op against the pair's
two rows, M=1, heads 16: fused 18.22 / 18.26 us against `mla_attn_paged_sink` 9.50 + expand 12.04 =
21.54 at window 0, and 16.90 / 17.01 against 8.69 + 12.04 = 20.73 at window 512.

## What the numbers say

1. **The launch is not where the step's time is, and the chain is.** A pass loses 42 launches and
   gains 84 that are plain where their predecessors were chained: chaining is decided by name, and
   the fused kernel's is not in `rad_kernel_chain`'s list (which holds `mla_attn_paged_sink_bf16`
   and `mla_expand_gate_had_quant_i8g`; `libr4d/r4d_rows.cpp`, `extern "C" int
   rad_kernel_chain`), so `select.cpp` never sets `chain`, the launcher's `r4dchain` arm is dead
   code — and unsafe as written anyway, because the fold reads the sink's scratch before
   `kvq_body<CH, true>` reaches `r4d_chain_wait()`. The stamps name the forms:
   `kv_sink_expand_gate_quantI12R4dUnchained` where the pair ran
   `kv_expand_gate_quantI8r4dchain` and `merge_sinkI8r4dchain`. The chained forms overlap a launch
   with the one in front; the unchained ones do not, which is the 0.36 ms of wall that the +0.02 ms
   of device time does not explain.
2. **The fold's work is dearer at one row and cheaper at eight.** The c8 pass-kind separates by layer
   kind, because 30 of its 42 layers are fused and 12 still run the pair: the 30 fused layers cost
   39.9 us of merge+expand in the base (merge 35.5 + expand 4.4, from the 42-layer figures with the
   12 pair layers' 8.75 + 6.1 taken out) against 35.6 us of fused kernel on the branch, i.e. 4.3 us
   a layer cheaper; the 12 pair layers are unchanged; the sink rows come in 18 us cheaper across the
   pass. That is the 146 us. At c1 the group's exclusive sum goes the other way, +88 us a pass
   (34.7 -> 36.8 us a layer), and the fused kernel's 25.4 us a layer is well above the 18.2 us the
   same op times in kbench.
3. **The kbench fixture does not stand in for the served pass.** The fixture row fixes M and the head
   count but not the context the sink runs at, and the fold walks the sink's splits, so its price
   follows the phase's context: at M=1 and 16 heads the row times the op at 18.22 us, where the c1
   pass-kind's fused kernel averages 25.4 us a layer, and the item's step is that pass. The header's
   bands ("3.1-3.4 us a layer faster at one to two rows", taken from the fixture) therefore say
   nothing about the step the 0.5-1.5 % was claimed for, and the row bound built on them inherits
   that.

## Why it is closed without code

CARDS.md §2: "the targeted kernel / op measurably faster in ms ... and no e2e regression beyond
noise: merge, even if e2e is noise. Otherwise write `closed/<ID>.md` with the numbers and the reason,
and close the card without merging code". The e2e clause fails by 1.4-2.6 % of the decode step on
the lane, reproducibly, and against the branch's own fold-off control; the kernel clause fails with
it, because the group the item targets costs 88 us more a pass at the served shape. The change
therefore had no configuration it could ship in. The review round's own finding named the mechanism
before the lane had a price for it ("the launch saved is paid for with a chain break at the same
point"); the lane's numbers are that price, and it is larger than the launch it saved.

The byte clause was not demonstrated either. On the `arrival5.7K` greedy case (64 generated tokens)
the two trees give different texts on every arm — base 4/4 arms 260 chars,
sha256 `466e53a2921d0216…`; branch 4/4 arms 284 chars, sha256 `5696132140a041e7…` — and it survives
`RADIANCE_HY4_SINK_FOLD=0` (whose stamps show the pair: `merge_sink` 14574 rows,
`kv_expand_gate_quant` 14574, `kv_sink_expand` 0) and `busy-prefill-chunk: 512` pinned on both trees.
The other three greedy cases are identical. So something in the change's range moves bytes where the
fold cannot reach; not chased here, because the revert removes it — but the item is BI, and a retry
has to show a fold-off arm byte-identical to base before it can claim the class.

## What a retry inherits

- **Chain the fused row or do not build it.** The item's price is dominated by the chain, not by the
  fold: 1270 -> 1144 chained launches a pass costs 0.36 ms where 3 -> 2 launches a layer saves
  nothing measurable. A fused row that can win has to have its kernel in `rad_kernel_chain`'s list
  (chaining is decided by name in `core/device/aql.cpp`) and fold *behind* `r4d_chain_wait()`,
  with the body then running unchained and `r4d_chain_done()` at the end;
  `tests/chain_scalar_audit.py` is the check (`r4d_chain.h` on what the acquire covers).
- **The fold walks the sink's splits, so its price follows them, and the sink's split law is the
  plugin's business only through the row bound.** Where the sink is split 8 ways (the window-512
  layers) the fold pays: c8's fused layers are exactly those, and the group is 146 us a pass
  cheaper. Where it is split 20 ways (the window-0 layers) the fold is dearer than the merge, and
  the c1 pass fuses both kinds. A retry should record the kbench row at the served pass's split
  count before setting `sink_fold_rows`, and should take the law from libr4d rather than keeping
  `kFoldSuperChunk` / `kFoldSplitsMax` in the plugin beside it.
- **The row bound was head-blind** (4 rows at window 0, 8 at 512) while the crossover is not: the
  earlier round measured the fold losing from three rows at 8 heads (TP4) and from two rows at 32
  heads, and the fixture that set the bound holds 8 and 32 heads only at M=1/17/64.
- **`arrival5.7K` is the case to explain** before the class can be claimed, on a fold-off arm.

## Seen and left alone

- The base's chained brackets overlap, so `agg.py`'s exclusive sum is not comparable across a change
  that drops chaining, and the group's union is not either while the base's first kernel spins on a
  launch outside the group. The like-for-like figures for a chain change are wall, device-busy union
  and the served step; the earlier review's "the fused launch costs 25.4 us where the two it
  replaces cost 19.2" reads the exclusive sum as if it were one.
- `docs/OPS.md`'s op reference and the three registries described the fused op as replacing "two
  ops" in one place and "three ops" in another (review round 2); moot now, but a retry should say
  which — the op replaces `merge_sink` + the expand, while the sink's rows stay a launch of the same
  op.
- `r4d_mla_sink.hip`'s sink launch and `merge_sink` are unchanged by the revert, and K-14's result
  still stands beside this one: the merge's body is 1.1-1.9 us of work inside a 15-19 us bracket,
  so the group's price at c1 is the launch and the chain, which is what this card tried to move and
  what it lost.
