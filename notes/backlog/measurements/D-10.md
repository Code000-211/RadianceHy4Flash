# D-10 measurement: `gemm_nt_f32` x staged transposed in LDS at verify heights (#48)

Measured per CARDS.md §6 step 3, against the merge bar in §2, with `K-52.md` as the model. The baseline is the default
branch with K-10 in (#221, `497c328c`, `K-10.md`): K-10 split the tile form's sums from its store, which removed most of
what the backlog's 33.3 µs a layer had charged to the x loads. Times are read from the `rule16` line of `--perff32`
(bf16 weight, as the served routers hold it) with the `rule` line (f32 weight) beside it, and from the served router
row in `rad-kbench`.

Two rounds. Round 1 measured `cc05324e`, which sent every verify height to the staged form; in the served router row
(x's rows contiguous, so x is staged 4 bytes at a time) it was 11–13 % faster up to 56 rows but no faster from 57.
Round 2 found that crossover and measured `803ffed7`, which keeps 8x1 above 56 rows when x is staged 4 bytes at a time.

## Why verify heights

Speculative decoding (DFly2 here) verifies several drafted tokens a sequence, so a verify step at a few sequences runs
the router GEMM at 17–63 rows. Hy4's router (`glm_router.weight`, N 512 K 3072, bf16 weight) runs replicated below
`GlmTp::kSplitRows` (256 rows), so its verify pass is N 512 at every TP width, with x's rows contiguous; from 256 rows
(prefill) it is column-split (N 256 a rank at TP 2, N 128 at TP 4). The change sends 17–63 rows to the staged form at
N ≥ 512 and at N 256–511 from 32 rows, except 57–63 rows staged 4 bytes at a time, which keep 8x1. Every other shape
keeps its form, so rows at N 128, below 32 rows at N 256, at 16 or 64 rows and the served row at 57–63 rows are
controls.

  gemm_nt_f32` at `--max-tok` 48, 52, 56, 60, 63, then `--perff32` at N 512 × M 48, 52, 56, 60, 63 and N 256 × M 48, 56, 63.
- **Round 2** (A = `f2a0c7ef`, B = `803ffed7`), A1 B1 B2 A2 A3 B3 B4 A4: card 0 `rad-kbench` at `--max-tok` 17, 24, 32,
  40, 48, 52, 56, 57, 60, 63, `--perff32` at N 256 and 512 × M 32, 48, 56, 57, 63, `--case gemm_nt_f32_bf16`; a TP 4
  profile-ops server (c1 and c8 probes) in A1 and B1; a TP 4 e2e server (greedy parity) in every session.
- **Round 1** (A = `f2a0c7ef`, B = `cc05324e`), A1 B1 B2 A2 A3 B3: card 0 `--perff32` over 27 shapes (N 128, 256, 512 ×
  M 16, 17, 24, 31, 32, 40, 48, 63, 64), `--case gemm_nt_f32_bf16`, `rad-kbench` (max-tok 63 and 48), a TP 4
  profile-ops server, then a TP 4 e2e server (greedy parity, then three probe rounds). The full `r4d_selftest --jobs 1`

Round 2 ran no end-to-end probes: the bound only returns 57–63 rows staged 4 bytes at a time to the 8x1 form, which is

## 1. The crossover (round 2, before vs `cc05324e`)

Round 1 had the served router row at 63 rows +2.6 % (every B run slower than every A run). Both forms take x in groups
of 8 rows, so 57–63 rows are one more group than 49–56. Below, staged 4 bytes at a time, the staged form is 11–12 %
faster at 48, 52 and 56 rows in every run, and from 60 rows it is no faster: its runs split between a few percent faster
and up to 3 % slower than every 8x1 run (medians +0.5 % at 60 rows, +0.3 % at 63). Staged 2 bytes at a time
(`--perff32`, pitch K+5) it stays 7–10 % faster at 60 and 63 rows in every run, at N 256 and 512. So the bound is on the 4-byte staging only, above 56 rows (7 groups of 8).
