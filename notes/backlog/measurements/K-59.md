## The change under test

Axes added, each reachable only where its kernel variant already exists, and each **bit-neutral**: the
alternative forms produce the same bytes, which is what the tests below check.

| row(s) | axis | values | what it selects |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mla_attn_paged_sink_bf16`, `mla_attn_sel_sink_bf16` | `hg` | 1, 2, 4 | how many sixteen-head groups a workgroup loads |
| `moe_gather_bf16`, `moe_gather_inv_bf16`, `moe_gather_inv_i8g` | `form` | 1, 2, 3 | which of the three existing gather kernels runs |
| `moe_gemm_iqr`, `moe_gemm_iqr_mod4` | `map` | 4, 8 | the run map or the packed map, over the same rows |
| `moe_gemm_i8a8(_mod4)`, `moe_gemm_w4a8i8(_mod4)` | `dmt` | 2, 4 | how many M tiles a block's down tile spans |

Every axis's `0` is the row's existing law, and the test pins (`g_sink_form`,
`r4d_moe_gemm_pin_down_mt`, the IQR map pin) still win over the axis, so neither a test nor a served
path changed behaviour. Not added: the sink's planned `rt` / `sw` / `kb` forms and the
`gemm_iqr_tiled` / `gemm_i8a8_tiled` tile arms — the kernels they would select between do not exist in
the tree (audit F2.2, F2.3, F7.1), so there is nothing to add an axis to.

spread). `rad-tune` prints a row's candidate block and then the row's summary line; the numbers below

Read the two tables against each other as **parity**: the same kernel runs in both trees on these rows
(the law's `mb=0`, which is the default in both), and the law's own median moves by −9 %…+3 % between the
trees and by −4 %…+3 % between the two runs of a single tree (`mla_project_kv` h16 absorb: 388.262 and
397.521 before, 353.187 and 393.338 after). That is the session-to-session noise of a busy machine, and
it is the size of every difference in this file. The change adds no instruction to a path that does not
take a new axis.

**The winners, and what they say.**

- **`mb` of `mla_project_kv` / `mla_expand_gate` — the item's own question** (never tuned at M 2048 ×
  16/32 heads): all five points walked on all six rows, and the law wins six of six in both trees. The
  margins are large where they matter: at h16 absorb the law is 19 % faster than its nearest rival
  (`mb=4`, 478.729 µs) and 133 % faster than `mb=8`; at h32 absorb 6 % against `mb=4`. The one row the
  law loses (`mla_expand_gate` h16 in the after runs) loses by 0.7 % to `mb=1`, inside that row's 18 %
  spread, and it loses to the law in the before runs. **There is no `mb` setting to take and no law to
  change: the Hy4 prefill rows are already at the fastest point the axis opens.**
- **sink `hg`**: the law's choice wins on every sink row. At heads=16 only `hg=1` is legal (2 and 4 are
  refused: the shape cannot fill them), so the axis is a no-op there. At heads=32 the law's 2 is
  confirmed twice: `hg=0` (the law) 10024.8 µs against `hg=2` 10098.1 µs at M=8192 (0.7 %), and the
  M=256 decode row (145.962 µs) is the noise-dominated one — the same candidate measured 138.9, 150.6
  and 160.8 µs in three runs of spreads 9–15 %.
- **`form` / `map` / `dmt` (the MoE rows)**: the container's declared graph at TP 2 reaches 44 `(kernel,
  shape)` pairs and **none of them is a MoE row** — a whole-graph `rad-tune` run (`--op` absent, 8471
  candidate rows walked before it was stopped) printed no `moe_` row either. So `rad-tune` cannot walk
  these three axes on this model today; their byte parity is checked by the selftest rows below, and
  their speed at Hy4 prefill shapes stays unmeasured until a MoE row is in the declared graph. That gap
  is reported here, not papered over.

## kbench, and why it is not the instrument here

```
gpu2 env RADIANCE_HOME=build/radiance_home ./build/bin/rad-kbench -m …/hy4-flash-iqr-v4-u3.rad \
  --home $RADIANCE_HOME --ref libref --op mla_attn_paged_sink --op mla_attn_sel_sink \
  --op mla_project_kv --op mla_expand_gate --op moe_gather --op moe_gemm_iqr --op moe_gemm_w4a8i8 \
  --max-tok 2048 --timing-only --bench-reps 20 --report …
```

The before tree's first report timed 836 cases but reached **6 of 645** `libr4d` rows, every one of
them at M ≤ 64: this container's declared graph at TP 2 has no prefill-M mla shape, and `--max-tok 2048`
changes the declare's `max_tok`, not the shapes the graph reaches. Its per-op medians therefore say
nothing about the M = 2048…8192 rows the item is about, while the tune report walks the graph's own
shapes and repeats each candidate nine times. The kbench pass was stopped after one report per tree —
20 minutes a report on cards other runs were queued for, for numbers the tune report already has. One
line of that report is worth keeping, because it is a finding about the harness itself:

```
| mla_attn_sel_sink (one case needs 13.91 GiB, over the 8.00 GiB budget -- raise --max-bytes to check it) | 4 |
```

Each compares the two maps' bytes against the run map's at the same routing (and the map row counts),
so "the axis takes either" means "either gives the same bytes". `moe_gemm_w4a8i8`'s `dmt` axis is
checked the same way in the same file (2 against 4 over the 32-row block's bytes, and `dmt=8` refused),
as is `mla_attn_paged_sink`'s `hg` axis. On the CPU side `tests/r4d_emu/test_moefuse.cpp` (188 cases,
the gather `form` axis) and `tests/r4d_emu/test_mla_sink.cpp` (the sink `hg` axis) cover the same
properties without a card; both pass.

## What this measurement concludes

1. The axes are in place, bit-neutral, and reachable from `rad-tune` / `RADIANCE_TUNE=`: the candidate
   listing shows `hg`, `form`, `map` and `dmt` at the Hy4 rows, `0` being the law.
2. The item's tuning question — `mb` at M 2048 × 32/16 heads — is answered: the law is the fastest of
   the five points on every row, in both trees. **No speedup is claimed for this change**, and no law
   changes with it.
3. What the change buys is what the item says it is for: the axes K-41, K-42 and K-51 need in order to
   tune these rows at all, and the sink `hg` axis walked on this model for the first time.
4. The merge bar asks for a measurably faster kernel/op; a tooling change whose new settings all default
   to the existing law cannot deliver one. The bar's "otherwise" (a `closed/` note, no code) would have
   thrown away the item's deliverable, so the deliverable and the measurement that shows its own gain
   is zero are both carried here, and the zero is stated plainly.
