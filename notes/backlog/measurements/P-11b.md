The served u3 stores the Engram table as E4M3 rows, so its rows take no round trip and the three planes were
pure arena; the two product planes are written only by the unfused `silu_mul` + `had_quant_act_i8g` pair, and
the served recipe folds the gate (`gemm_nt_q_gated`), so nothing named either of them. The arena line drops by
0.03 and 0.10 GiB, which is the two sums within the line's two-decimal rounding (39.13 MiB = 0.038 GiB,
96.50 MiB = 0.094 GiB): the arena is the *peak live* of the packed set, so a plane that was live everywhere
returns its bytes to the packing and the peak falls by its size.

**The fused gate is the reason `arch/common/rad_fp8.h` is in this diff.** With a plane dropped and the block
untouched, the intermediate dump lost the fold — `trunk_codes.gated.6912.128.q8` (13.50 MiB) and its `.s8`
went dead beside it, because `GateQuantFP8::declare` refused a wire with no bf16 plane even on the row that
writes the codes alone (`want_x` false, `rad_op_writes(b, op, wr, want_x ? 3 : 2)`), so the dense MLP fell back
to the unfused pair. Asking for the plane where a form names it — and only there, with `want_x && !a.x` still
`RAD_E_INVAL` — gave the fold back; the numbers above are with the fold.

## The 10 planes that stay, and why each one does

Read from the same dump; each chased to the code that asks for it. `hyv4flash_arch_test`'s
`no_transient_is_declared_that_no_op_reads` pins the same residue per plan, by name and by bytes, over six
plans — bf16, int8 trunk, int8 experts, IQR experts, two ranks, and `served_u3_encs`, the served recipe with
its Engram table at E4M3. The served plan pins four of the five rows above (`moe.expert_ff.q/.s`,
`moe.latent.q/.s`, 110 592 bytes at the fixture's geometry); it does not pin `moe.expert_ff`, because at that
geometry the expert gate is not served the fused gated quantiser and `silu_mul` still writes the plane — the
plane's unread state is the rig's dump's fact, in the list above, and the test says so rather than listing a
plane it cannot reach.

| plane (TP2 / TP4) | why it is still declared |
|---|---|
| `moe.expert_ff` 48 / 96 MiB | the routed arm's bf16 product. `silu_mul` writes it for bf16 experts (`rad_block_moe_fp8.h`, the `bf16` arm of `GateQuantFP8::declare`) and the rotated chain writes it whether or not a consumer asked; the wire that declares it cannot see a container's `expert_bf16` mix |
| `moe.expert_ff.q` / `.s` 24 MiB + 768 KiB / 48 + 1.5 MiB | its E4M3 codes. `down_codebook_fp8` / `down_supported` (`rad_block_moe_fp8.h`) ask for them — the mixed recipe of an IQR gate_up behind a w6 down with them — while no op of an int8 plan names them |
| `moe.latent.q` / `.s` 3 MiB + 96 KiB / 12 + 384 KiB | same: `MoeFP8::declare` refuses a wire whose latent carries no codes; its read is the rotated int8 pair |
| `ix.stage` 768 KiB / 2.25 MiB | staged raw keys of the closing group; issued only when the library does not serve the fused key side (`qsa_work_pool_tail`), which the wire cannot know for a layer's geometry |
| `sampler.cand_gathered/pairs/idx/val` 8 / 12 MiB | the sampler declares its ops with no operand lists at all (`core/sample/sampler.cpp`), so no plan can name them; a core change, outside this card |

The four code planes and `moe.expert_ff` together are 79.84 MiB of the 84.59 at TP2 and 157.88 of the 172.12 at
TP4: they are the card's remaining sum, and taking them means changing what `MoeFP8` requires of a quantised
expert's wire, not what the arch declares — `quantised_experts_are_served_clamped` and
`quantised_experts_are_sliced_at_more_ranks` are the tests of that rule and they refuse a wire without the
pair, which is why a first attempt at dropping them in this round was put back.

The issue quotes 14 planes and 115 MiB at TP2 / 255 MiB at TP4 (from #81). The dump at the merge base counts 15
and 123.72 / 268.62 MiB; the difference is the sampler's four planes and `ix.stage` (8.75 MiB at TP2), which the
issue's list does not carry. The dump is the authority here.

## One config, both arms — and one thing this measurement does not claim

An earlier window ran each arm with the `scripts/backlog/nospec.yaml` **of its own tree**, which is the same
file in both trees and asks for `max-model-len: 65536`. The base boot came up clamped —
`I --max-model-len 8192 [models.hy4-flash.max-model-len]`, `kv cache 1.10 GiB … backs 8 sequences all at
8192 tokens` — while the head boot served the requested 65536 (`kv cache 7.33 GiB`, `9 checkpoint slots a
sequence` instead of 5). Its rows are therefore not comparable: the arms differed in context length, KV size
and checkpoint slots, not only in the plan. Those rows are left out here, and the quoted lane writes
one value into one YAML both arms read.

What the discarded window does support is an **observation, not a claim**: on this rig at this cap, the
merge base could not afford the configured 65,536-token worst case where the head could. Nothing here says
how much of that is the freed arena and how much is the startup's own auto-sizing reacting to it; the
engine logs no clamp line, so it is not measured. It is written down because a reviewer may see the same
thing and wonder.

## What the numbers say, and what they do not

- **Measured:** the plan's charged set and the arena it packs to, both shapes, card-less, re-taken on the
  current head and quoted line for line from the named files; the startup arena line and its two levels,
  nine boots an arm; `c1` and `c8` throughput and the whole TTFT array of every `c8` run, three windows of
  three interleaved runs an arm, two of them with B booted first; byte-identical greedy output on all
  eighteen runs; the two binaries by `sha256sum` in every boot line.
- **The merge bar:** lossless — the output is byte-identical; the item's target is memory, and it moved by
  the amount the plan says it should (39.13 MiB charged away at TP2/2048, 96.50 at TP4/8192, and 19.56 MiB
  off the 1024-row level of the lane's arena, both the plan's arithmetic and the boot agreeing to the byte).
  End to end nothing is claimed: the nine within-pair differences average −0.11 % on `c1` and −0.17 % on
  `c8`, with a sign that flips between windows and a spread (−0.41 … +0.09 tok/s, −3.24 … +2.56) larger than
  either mean and comparable to what one arm shows within one window (0.22 and 5.2 at their smallest). The
  0.29 % `c1` gap of the A-first first window is not repeated in the two B-first ones (+0.02 %, +0.07 %),
  so it belongs to the boot order and the rig, not to the change. A kernel-level ms gain is not what this
  item targets: no op got faster, one fewer plane gets reserved.
