# X-05 design: KV streaming for the 12 sparse layers

Design card of
[X-05](../optimisation-backlog.md#x-05-kv-streaming-for-the-12-sparse-layers-research) (`#133`, whose build card is
`#134` and whose blocker `#8` — the kvstream module — is in the tree at `489fd4f4`). Lane: none — this note is
arithmetic over numbers the tree and the audits already hold, and it needs no card. Agent: Opus 5.5, thinking
medium. It plans two build cards, X-05a (the Hy4 shape at the ring's bounds: the tests and the recipe's numbers;
no gain on its own) and X-05b (the measurement), and names a third, X-05c, that only opens if the measurement says
so. §8 holds the three bodies.

**What this note found, in one paragraph.** The mechanism the item asks for is already in the tree:
`arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash.cpp:1603` sets `kv_stream_ok`, and the sparse latent group is stored streamed and
resolved between the indexer's selection and `mla_attn_sel_sink` (`arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash_attn.h:336-337,484,762`;
commit `489fd4f4`, `docs/ARCHITECTURES.md:3434`). What is *not* in the tree is the item's number: there is no
Hy4-flash measurement of the streamed path on the real model at any shape, and the two quantities the trade
turns on — how much of a decode step's indexer selection churns, and what a 4,608-byte `kv_resolve` copy
achieves on the link — have never been read on Hy4. This note does the link budget against the expert misses
the freed VRAM returns (§3): at 4 x 60K the ring is 648 MiB and gives back 2.46 GiB = 1,759 units, whose misses
at today's hit rate are 279 MB a step; the units bought back are worth 157-240 MB of that, and the resolve
costs 9-113 MB an interval depending on the churn. So the lever is byte-positive on every arm of the budget;
its *size* is 0 to ~20 % of a decode step. The item's premise ("~2.5 GiB = 1,800 units back to the experts, at
the price of resolve copies on the same link") is right in kind and in size. What the item does not say, and
what this note adds, is three things: the resolve's price is set by one number nobody has read on this
architecture (its achieved bandwidth at a 4,608-byte block); the one measured instance of this trade on the
same plugin gave back 3.58 GiB a card and bought *nothing* end to end at 262K tokens (GUIDE §4.4.1), so the
gain is only where VRAM is what fails to start; and at 1M tokens the same ring is not a trade but the
difference between serving and refusing (11.80 GiB returned, §3.4). The card is therefore a measurement card,
not a port.

What it decides: §0.

## 0. The decisions, one by one

**D1 — X-05 is a measurement card, not a port.** The streamed store, the resolve, the ring, the stage and the
mirror for a Hy4 sparse group are shipped (`489fd4f4`); the plugin's probe says so. Rewriting the item as
"make hyv4flash stream" would re-land code that exists. §11 rewrites the build card to what is missing: the
Hy4 shape at the ring's bounds (tests and bring-up lines) and the measurement that decides whether the lever
is a default or an option.

**D2 — only the sparse latent group streams; the sliding latent, the indexer keys and the tail stay
resident.** `kv_stream_group` picks one group — the widest full group that owns bytes, is not dense-read and
declares the streamed ops (`core/kvstream/plan.cpp:26-45`). At Hy4's geometry that is `kv_latent_sparse`
(12 layers of 1,152 B a token = 13,824 B a token, page `55296`). The sliding latent is a 512-token window
(17.7 MiB) and declares no streamed ops; `kv_index_keys` is `rad_kv_group_dense_read`
(`hyv4flash.cpp:848`) because the indexer scores the whole history every step. Both stay as they are.

**D3 — it stays opt-in (`--kv-resident 0` by default) until the measurement lands.** §3 shows the bytes are
in its favour on every arm and that its magnitude is anywhere between a wash and ~20 % of a decode step — and
§3.2 records that the one measured instance of the trade on this plugin (Flash-Next, 262K, 3.58 GiB given
back) was a wash, because that step's link had headroom. A default cannot be chosen from a model.

**D4 — the free VRAM is real; the *ring the flag carves* is priced on `max-num-seqs` and that must be
documented, not changed.** `kvstream_ceiling_blocks` is `max_seqs x (1 + n_spec) x sel_pitch` blocks
(`core/kvstream/plan.cpp:71-76`), and it is a *correctness* bound: the resolve's selection union must fit or
`ctl[3]` latches and fails the request (`docs/KV-STREAMING.md` §3.4). So the ceiling is not a knob to
undercut. But it means the ring is `2048 x (1 + n_spec)` tokens *a sequence slot*, and the stage is one layer
of every context the recipe admits: at `--max-num-seqs 256` (the default, `core/rad_core.h:887`) Hy4 would
carve a 7.25 GB ring (6.75 GiB, more than the whole live set of a four-sequence 60K workload) and want a
17.7 GB stage, which no 32 GB card carries; at 4 it is 648 MiB and 264 MiB. **A streamed Hy4 recipe must set
`--max-num-seqs` to the concurrency it serves.** The startup receipt already names all three
(`core/kvstream/bringup.cpp:145-172`: the mirror a rank, the ring's blocks and the one-layer stage's), and
`docs/GUIDE.md` §4.4.1 already states the cost model — the ring at the group's per-token bytes, the stage as
one layer of the retained context, the host copy at `max-num-seqs` x `max-model-len` tokens a rank, and the
pool as their `max`. So what is missing there is Hy4's row (13,824 / 4,608 / 768 B, and the two numbers at
256), not the rule.

**D5 — the binding resource above ~500K tokens is the host mirror, not VRAM.** The mirror is reserved for
`max-num-seqs x max-model-len` tokens a rank (`docs/KV-STREAMING.md` §3.8), and Hy4's sparse latent is
13,824 B a token, 2.1x Flash-Next's 6,655 B. One 1M-token sequence is 13.8 GB a rank; four are 55 GB, which
the four ranks of `iks` (112 GB total) do not have. X-05's long-context claim is therefore one long sequence a
rank until a replicated group's mirror is shared (§10, X-05c).

**D6 — X-05 does not depend on X-01; the two are exclusive on the same group and complementary by
workload.** The item says "Deps X-01 first". X-01 (the host tier for idle conversations) and streaming cannot
run together: `kvstream_prefix_host_off` turns the prefix cache's host tier off when a group streams
(`core/kvstream/plan.h:110-113`, `docs/KV-STREAMING.md` §3.8). They answer different workloads — X-01 a
multi-turn agent with idle sessions (640 units a session, no decode-time link cost), X-05 one live
long context (2.46 GiB at 4 x 60K, at a link cost). The dependency is dropped in §11's card.

**D7 — the floor stays 32,768 tokens, and Hy4's numbers say it is not generous.** The floor is the token
count below which a step's misses cost more than the ring gives back (`core/kvstream/plan.h:95-98`,
`docs/KV-STREAMING.md` §3.9: measured break-even near 24,576 tokens on Flash-Next). Hy4 frees 13,824 B a
token and pays `12 x P(selected) x 1152` B a step a token, `P = 2048/ctx`. At the 64K history §3.9 measured
its floor at, that is 432 B a token, a freed/resolve ratio of 32; Flash-Next frees 6,555 B a token and pays
13 x (512/16,384) x 516 = 210 B, a ratio of 31. The two are indistinguishable, so a floor inherited from
Flash-Next (32,768 tokens against its measured 24,576) transfers to Hy4 as it stands, and lowering it would
be the wrong way.

**D8 — X-07 (an int8 latent) divides both sides of the budget by two and changes no decision here.** It
halves the freed VRAM (2.46 GiB -> 1.23 GiB at 4 x 60K) and halves the resolve's bytes (4,608 -> 2,304 B a
layer page), so the ratio and the capability question are unchanged; the ring's floor stays in tokens
(32,768) and therefore halves in bytes. X-05 is measured in bf16 first; X-07 re-runs the same ledger.

**D9 — the resolve's achieved bandwidth is the number to read, before tok/s.** §3.3: at the doc's 0.8 us a
miss the break-even churn is 0.29, at a bulk h2d rate it is beyond total churn. The card reads
`profile-ops`'s `kv_resolve_kernel` row and the report's `misses`/`largest launch union`, not only the token
rate, so the next run of this ledger needs no new measurement.

## 1. What is at HEAD, and what the item's premise gets wrong

Three statements in the item and its audit are stale at HEAD:

| claim | where | at HEAD |
|---|---|---|
| "hyv4flash does not set `kv_stream_ok`" | `audit/E-memory-placement-kv.md:157-158,483-485`, item X-05 | `arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash.cpp:1603` sets it; the sparse latent declares `kv_store_streamed` and `kv_resolve` |
| "KV streaming exists for QSA/GLM sparse attention" | same | `docs/ARCHITECTURES.md:3434-3448` documents Hy4's streamed group; `489fd4f4` landed it |
| GUIDE's model table: GLM-5.3 "declare and issue are tested, not served here"; "all other architectures: no streamed store and no `kv_resolve`" | `docs/GUIDE.md` §4.4.1 | Hy4-flash is a third row: declared, linked, and served on the mini model |

What the shipped path does, so the rest of this note can refer to it (all in `arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash_attn.h`):

- the store is `kv_store_streamed` when the group streams (`:336-337`): the host mirror always, and the ring
  slot where the block is resident, behind the stage wait; a staged (prefill) pass stores twice and never
  resolves (`:617-621`);
- a pass bound to the ring resolves the selection between `qsa_select` and `mla_attn_sel_sink`
  (`:484`, `:762`) with `sel_pitch = ix_group_topk` (512), then `kv_stage_next` follows the layer (`:794`);
- at or below the dense limit (2,048 tokens) the selection is every closed group, so the result is the dense
  one and the dense kernel's block ids are the ring's slots.

The one measured run is on `gpu-mini` (`docs/ARCHITECTURES.md:3554-3558`): a 60,000-token prompt with
`--kv-resident 32768`, a ring of 8,192 blocks under a 15,104-block context, 48 decode steps resolving 94
times with about 25,700 misses, and output bit-identical to the resident run at TP1 and TP2 and with the FP8
indexer. That is 273 misses a resolve over 512 claims — a 53 % churn, which is what random weights give when
the ring covers 54 % of the context and nothing repeats. It says the mechanism works; it says nothing about
Hy4 with trained weights, and nothing about the real model's 12 sparse layers, TP2 or TP4.

## 2. The geometry (per rank, TP2 and TP4 alike)

The latent groups are declared `rad_kv_group_replicated` (`hyv4flash.cpp:1213-1215`): MLA keeps one KV head,
so every rank holds the whole latent and TP splits heads, not cache. A streamed group's *pool* is not
replicated (`core/kvstream/carve.cpp:113`), but its mirror and its ring bytes are per rank and identical
across ranks.

| group | rows | per token | page (block) | at 60K a sequence | at 1M a sequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `kv_latent_sparse` (bf16, 12 layers) | latent 512 + rope 64, one head | **13,824 B** | 4 tokens = **55,296 B** (4,608 B a layer) | 829 MB | **13.8 GB** |
| `kv_latent_sliding` (bf16, 30 layers) | same row, window 512 | 34,560 B | 16 tokens | 17.7 MB (window-bounded) | 17.7 MB |
| `kv_index_keys` (bf16 or fp8, 12 layers) | one pooled key, head 16 (17 at fp8) | 768 B (456 B at fp8) | 4 tokens = 3,072 B | 46 MB | 768 MB |

Row bytes are `2 x head_dim` (`abi/rad_builder.h:69-98`), and the plugin declares `head_dim = (kv_lora +
qk_rope)/2` (`hyv4flash.cpp:706`), which is why the latent's row is 576 values = 1,152 B a token a layer.
The item's 13,824 B a token, the audit's "6.75 GiB at 8 x 65K" (`audit/E:124`) and the ARCHITECTURES line's
"55.3 MB a sequence of 4K tokens" (`:3434`) all check out against the code; there is no arithmetic to fix.

## 3. The link budget against expert misses

### 3.1 The ledger

One decode step of `n` sequences, each with `1 + n_spec` rows (a verify pass has one a proposal), and `h` the
routed-expert hit rate the audit measures (`audit/E` §2.3). `n_spec` is resolved to the drafter's own depth
before the pools are probed (`core/engine_bringup.cpp:1332-1341`), 5 for the container's DFly2; the ring without
speculation is the floor itself, 432 MiB, which frees 2.67 GiB instead of 2.46.

| term | expression | 4 x 60K, depth 5 |
|---|---|---|
| live latent in VRAM | `n x ctx x 13,824` | 3.09 GiB |
| ring the flag carves | `max(32,768 tokens, n x (1+n_spec) x 512 x 4 tokens)` | 648 MiB (49,152 tokens) |
| stage (one layer deep, `kv_pool_bytes`'s max) | `n x ceil(ctx/4) x 4,608 B` | 264 MiB |
| **freed for the experts** | `(live - max(ring, stage)) / 1.43 MiB` | **2.46 GiB = 1,759 units** |
| routed demand a step | `n x 41 x 16` | 2,624 units |
| today's miss traffic a step | `demand x (1-h) x 1.43 MiB`, `h = 0.929` | 186 units = **279 MB (10.1 ms)** |
| the freed units buy | `dh x demand x 1.43 MiB`, `dh` = 4.0-8.3 points (capped 6.1) | 105-160 units = **157-240 MB (5.7-8.7 ms)** |
| the resolve costs | `n x 12 x 512 x c x 4,608 B`, `c` the selection churn | **9-113 MB (0.3-4.1 ms bulk, 1.6-19.7 ms at 0.8 us a miss)** |
| its launches | 12 x ~4 us | 48 us |

`1.43 MiB` is the unit (`audit/E` §2.1), `27.6 GB/s` the link's measured staged/in-place rate (same
section), and `0.8 us a miss` is `docs/KV-STREAMING.md` §3.9's Flash-Next figure, which for Hy4's 4,608-byte
layer page is 5.8 GB/s if a miss's cost is latency-bound. The churn `c` is the fraction of a row's 512
entries whose block is not in the ring: 0.08 is the trained-shape anchor (Flash-Next's "tens a launch" of
513), 0.53 the random-weight anchor of gpu-mini's run above, 1.00 every entry.

Two things about the count. The *claims* a launch makes are `n x (1+n_spec) x 12 x 512`, which is why the ring
is priced with `1+n_spec`; the *misses* are distinct blocks, because the resolve bumps a slot once a call
however many rows name it (`docs/KV-STREAMING.md` §3.4, `core/engine.cpp:1868` counts placed blocks). So
speculation makes the ring bigger, not the traffic.

### 3.2 The anchors: what a unit is worth, and what the same trade measured

| anchor | what it fixes |
|---|---|
| `audit/E` §2.3, Hy4 N4 16K -> 60K: 18,017 -> 16,229 units, hit 0.971 -> 0.929, 190 -> 148 tok/s | the value of a unit on *this* model: 4.2 points of hit are 22 %, i.e. 6.3 ms of a 27 ms step. The misses' own bytes (186 -> 122 units a step = 96 MB = 3.5 ms) and the longer selection's KV reads (7 -> 28 MB a step = 0.8 ms) explain 4.3 of the 6.3; the rest is attention over a longer selection and prefix evictions. So ~1.5 ms a point, and 1,826 units are worth 6.3 ms |
| the same two rows as a slope | 2.3 points a 1,000 units |
| X-01: "640 units (3 points)" per idle 65K session | 4.7 points a 1,000 units |
| `docs/GUIDE.md` §4.4.1's Flash-Next measurement: 2 x 262K-token conversations on 2 x 16 GB at TP2, 3.58 GiB a card given back, streaming off -> on: decode 39.89 -> 39.78 and 38.60 -> 39.07 tok/s, prefill +1-2 %, output identical | the closest analogue of this exact trade: its end-to-end decode effect was **inside noise**, because that step's link had headroom and moving bytes from one side of it to the other changed nothing |

At 1,759 units the 4.2 points the span lost are recovered 1.0-2.0 times over (4.0-8.3 points, capped near 6.1 by
a ~0.99 ceiling), which in the audit's own currency is 6.0-9.2 ms of a 27 ms step. The audit's E-C5 estimate is
"~2.5 GiB = 1,800 units (8 points)" (`audit/E:486`), the top of that band. Two corrections follow. The ledger's
upper arm is as large as the *entire* measured residency effect of the span, so the honest expectation is its
lower arm, ~6 ms returned for 0.3-4 ms spent; and the Flash-Next row says that a gain of that size can still
be invisible end to end when the link is not the constraint. At this shape it is: 279 MB of expert misses a
step is 37 % of a 27 ms step, and the audit measures the link at 50-65 % busy at many long sessions
(`audit/E:224`). What the measurement has to show is therefore the resolve's own row and the misses it
removes, with tok/s not worse — CARDS.md §2's third clause, not a 20 % decode promise.

### 3.3 The break-even, and what it means

In bytes: `c* = returned / 113 MB` = 1.4-2.1, i.e. **a total churn still moves fewer bytes through the
resolve than the experts give back**. In time at the doc's per-miss cost: `c* = 157 MB / (113 MB / 0.8 us a
miss)` = **0.29**. So:

- at Flash-Next's churn (0.08) the lever wins on either rate, by 5-8 ms of a 27 ms step;
- at gpu-mini's random-weight churn (0.53) it wins at the bulk rate (2.2 ms spent for 5.7 returned) and loses
  by ~5 ms at the per-miss one;
- at a total churn it wins in bytes (113 MB for 157) and loses in time (19.7 ms for 5.7).

**The number that decides is the resolve's achieved bandwidth at Hy4's block size, and the second is the
selection's churn.** Neither has been read on this architecture. That is D9 and it is what X-05b measures.

### 3.4 Where the lever is decisive instead: 0.5M-1M

The same ledger at one 1M-token sequence, TP2 (`--max-num-seqs 1`):

| term | value |
|---|---|
| live latent | 12.87 GiB a card (the X-07 item's measured figure for the whole KV at 1M, TP2, is 14.46 GiB) |
| ring (floor 32,768 tokens) / stage (one layer of 1M) | 432 MiB / 1,099 MiB -> pool **1,099 MiB** |
| freed for the experts | **11.80 GiB = 8,451 units** |
| resolve, one row a layer | 12 x 512 x c x 4,608 B = 28 MB x `c` a step |
| host mirror | 13.8 GB **a rank** |

This is not a trade, it is the difference between starting and not: the X-07 item's measured figure is that
14.46 GiB of KV a card at TP2 "leaves the experts 9.33 GiB and fails to start with the TP2 recipe's host
pool" (`notes/backlog/optimisation-backlog.md:977`). Streamed, the same request fits in 1.1 GiB of VRAM plus
the resident indexer, and the experts get 8,451 units back — the largest single lever in the backlog, and the
one the item does not claim. At four 1M sequences the pool is 4,395 MiB and the mirror 55 GB, so four long
requests a rank are out of reach until X-05c (§10).

### 3.5 Prefill pays, but not much

A 60K prefill writes the mirror (829 MB) and stages the retained context one layer at a time (829 MB), 60 ms
of link against a prefill of tens of seconds; at 1M it is 27.6 GB, ~1 s against a prefill of minutes. The
stage's VRAM is the pool's `max` term, which is why it appears in §3.1's ledger at all. TTFT parity is in the
card's merge bar, not here.

## 4. What does not change

- **The ops.** `kv_store_streamed` and `kv_resolve` take Hy4's shape without a change: `block_bytes` is the
  group's per-layer page (4,608 B), `sel_pitch` is `ix_group_topk` (512) and `sel` is already a block-id table
  because `mla_attn_sel_sink` reads one. Nothing in `libr4d/` moves.
- **The attention kernels.** A ring-bound pass reads its slots through exactly the ids it read before; a pass
  at or below the dense limit is unchanged (the selection is every closed group). Parity is a claim to
  re-measure, not a kernel change.
- **The indexer.** Dense-read, resident, and the fp8 page is orthogonal (`489fd4f4`'s second half).
- **The draft model.** DFly2's draft rides the target's sparse KV — its verify rows are rows of the same group,
  which is why the ring's ceiling counts `1 + n_spec`.
- **The prefix cache.** Its host tier is off while a group streams (D6); VRAM hits still work.

## 5. Failure modes and the tests that hold them

Shipped and tested (`tests/hyv4flash_arch_test.cpp`, `tests/r4d_emu/test_kvres.cpp`, `libref/ref_kvstream.cpp`,
`r4d_selftest --case kv_resolve`): the order (store < select < resolve < attention), the entry count operand,
a pass below the dense limit still selecting on the ring, a staged pass storing twice and never resolving,
the probe's `kv_stream_ok`, hits/ascending misses/eviction/free-before-live/second chance/saturation/overflow,
and the copy.

Two gaps the Hy4 shape exposes, both X-05a's:

1. **`test_kvres.cpp` runs pitch 513, blocks of 8 bytes.** Hy4 is pitch 512, 4,608-byte layer pages, block 4,
   twelve bound layers, bf16 rows: the same code, but the launch's claim list, the 4-word alignment of a
   4,608-byte copy and the row's scratch padding are untested at this shape. A fixture costs a table row.
2. **The ceiling against the report.** `stream_report` prints `largest launch claims` and says a value above
   `max_seqs x sel_pitch` means the derivation is wrong (`core/engine.cpp:1897-1902`). Nothing asserts that
   for a Hy4-shaped ring with `n_spec > 0`, where the claims are `n x 6 x 512`; `sched_test` should.

## 6. The measurement plan (what X-05b runs)

The merge bar of a lossless item (CARDS.md §2) over the same lane, `local` TP2 at the 210-250 W cap, `iks` for
TP4:

1. **Parity.** The same prompts, greedy and 8 logprob values, bit-identical across `--kv-resident 0`,
   `32768` and `49152`, at TP1 and TP2, with DFly2 on and off, and across a prefix-cache hit. gpu-mini showed
   identity at TP1/TP2; the real model at TP2 with the real indexer has not been shown.
2. **The ledger.** Per arm: the bring-up lines (ring blocks/tokens, stage blocks, mirror bytes a rank), the
   request-end `kv streaming: group ... N resolves, M lookups, P misses, largest launch union/misses/claims`,
   `radiance:kv_stream_misses_total` / `kv_stream_bytes_total`, `profile-ops`'s `kv_resolve_kernel` ms a step,
   `/stats`'s VRAM units, and tok/s + TTFT.
3. **The arms** at N4 D60K, three interleaved runs each: off; `--kv-resident 32768` (ring 432 MiB, freed
   2.67 GiB); `--kv-resident 49152` (the item's ~2.5 GiB, freed 2.46 GiB). Then N8 D48K with DFly2 (freed
   4.9 GiB, the X-01 longctx shape) and one 1M-token request (the capability arm of §3.4).
4. **What the numbers decide.** If the resolve's bytes/step over its own device time is within ~2x of a bulk
   h2d copy, the lever is a default candidate (D3 lifts); if it is at 0.8 us a miss, it stays an option for
   long single contexts only. Either way the churn `c` and the resolve's rate go into
   `notes/backlog/measurements/X-05.md`, so the next ledger is arithmetic.

## 7. Risks, and what would close the item

- **The churn could be much higher than Flash-Next's.** A Hy4 row picks 512 of ~15,000 blocks at 60K where
  Flash-Next picks 512 of 16,384 — about the same density at the same context, so about the same churn is
  expected; a much higher one would be a finding about the indexer, not about streaming.
- **The end-to-end gain can be inside noise.** GUIDE §4.4.1's Flash-Next measurement is the same trade with a
  3.58 GiB gift and a flat decode. That is an acceptable merge (CARDS.md §2's third clause is "not worse"), but
  it means the card must land the op-level rows, not a decode percentage; and if Hy4's link has headroom at
  4 x 60K too, the item's honest gain is the 1M capability (D5, §3.4) and the VRAM returned, not tok/s.
- **Host RAM.** 13.8 GB a rank for one 1M sequence (D5); the gate names `max-model-len` and `max-num-seqs`
  when it refuses.
- **A default that cannot be chosen from a model.** D3 keeps it off; the measurement (or X-05c) lifts it.
- **X-07 landing first** halves both sides; the ledger is unchanged (D8), but the 1M arm's freed VRAM halves to
  5.9 GiB, which is still decisive.
- The item closes when `notes/backlog/measurements/X-05.md` holds the arms above with output parity, and the
  docs rows (GUIDE's table, ARCHITECTURES' stale sentence, `audit/E`'s E-C5) are corrected.
