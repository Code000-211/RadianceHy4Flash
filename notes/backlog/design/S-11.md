# S-11 design: runahead / dynamic micro-batch interleaving for offloaded MoE decode

Design card of [S-11](../optimisation-backlog.md) (issue #304), the Bevy schedule
(arXiv:2611.04794): cut a decode step into two micro-batches, run micro-batch A through layer `l`'s
router to expose its *exact* missing experts, start those copies on the prefetch DMA queue, and let
micro-batch B's GPU work at the same layer hide them; merge back into one batch on layers with few
misses so the trunk weights are not read twice.

The item's Verify clause asks for a trace-based overlap simulation on `RADIANCE_ROUTE_TRACE` data
(c4/c8, `nospec` and `coupled03`/DFly2) proving a net wall-time reduction after the duplicated
shared-weight reads, with build cards gated on a clear win. This note is that simulation: the fork's
cost is measured on a lane rather than modelled, the trace supplies the routing, and every number is
reproducible from [`S-11-tools/runahead.py`](S-11-tools/runahead.py) (`--self-test` covered) and the
raw lines of [`notes/backlog/measurements/S-11.md`](../measurements/S-11.md).

Machine: `iks`, 2 x Radeon AI PRO R9700 (`gfx1201`, 32 GB), TP2, u3 container, 210 W cap, two clean
runs of each shape and one traced run of each. Every number below is from that lane and that cap.

## 0. Verdict

**No build card on the u3 container.** On every shape measured at TP2 the fork costs more than the
stall it can hide, by a factor of 6 to 20 (11.3 / 0.56 at the worst, 20.9 / 3.53 at the best):

| shape (TP2, u3) | step | misses a step | link time | stall | the fork costs | net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| c4 nospec | 17.78 ms | 16.4 | 1.08 ms | 0.56 ms | **+11.3 .. 12.7 ms** (2 x c2 - c4) | -10.7 .. -12.1 ms |
| c8 nospec | 23.87 ms | 27.6 | 1.82 ms | 0.94 ms | **+11.7 ms** (2 x c4 - c8) | -10.7 .. -10.8 ms |
| c4 DFly2 | 30.14 ms | 50.0 | 3.30 ms | 1.70 ms | **+14.0 .. 17.2 ms** | -12.3 .. -15.5 ms |
| c8 DFly2 | 35.41 ms | 103.9 | 6.85 ms | 3.53 ms | **+20.9 .. 28.9 ms** | -17.4 .. -25.4 ms |

The miss counts are the clean runs' engine counters (§1). The traced runs read the same shapes a
little differently - 22.7 and 22.3 misses a step at nospec c4/c8, 85.9 at DFly2 pooled (§4) - and
either way the fork is 6 to 20 times the whole stall, so the net column's sign does not depend on
which reading is used.

The reason is structural, not a tuning problem. A decode pass at TP2 costs a fixed ~12.2 ms before
its first row is added: over c2/c4/c8, `T(n) = 12.20 + 1.50 n` ms (fitted), and the two direct fork
measurements, 11.72 ms at c8 and 12.67 ms at c4 (11.65 and 11.29 in the second run), are that same
fixed pass. Splitting a step in two pays it twice, at every height. A DFly2 step is two passes (draft
and verify), so its fork is 21-29 ms.

What the schedule can hide is the stall of the step's misses: 0.6-0.9 ms at the nospec shapes and
3.5 ms at c8 DFly2, and at DFly2 the stall is measured rather than modelled - turning W2 off at the
same shape costs 3.5-4.0 ms a step (39.12 ms against 35.66 / 35.17) over 140.8 misses, ~25-28 us a
miss, which is [W2-prototype.md](../measurements/W2-prototype.md) §5 re-measured. The item's
headline, "hides PCIe expert-miss latency (7.3 ms/step on u3)", is the *link* time of those misses
(103.9 x 66 us = 6.9 ms here), not the stall; and W2, which is shipped, already takes that stall
inside the pass, at 25-28 us a miss, without reading the trunk twice.

What can pay is narrower, and §4 sizes it: a *per-layer* fork (the item's "merges back ... on layers
with few misses", taken to its limit) costs one extra layer pass, and it buys only that layer's own
misses. A layer has to miss 8.4 times a step (nospec) or 18 times (DFly2) to beat its share of the
measured fork; the hottest layer of the c8 nospec step misses 1.08 times, the hottest of the DFly2
run 3.35 times. So 0 of 41 layers are worth splitting on u3, in either container.

On u4, where the item puts the miss bill at 32-36 ms/step, the whole-step form turns over at the
nospec shapes (§7 works the threshold out) and stays negative at DFly2. No u4 container exists on
this machine or on `iks`, so §7 states the measurement that opens the card instead of guessing.

## 1. What is hidden today, what is left, and why prediction is not the way

| shape (TP2, u3) | step | routed units/step | misses/step | hit | link time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| c2 nospec | 14.88 ms | 1327 | 19.2 | 0.985 | 1.27 ms |
| c4 nospec | 17.78 ms | 2391 | 16.4 | 0.993 | 1.08 ms |
| c8 nospec | 23.87 ms | 4099 | 27.6 | 0.993 | 1.82 ms |
| c2 DFly2 | 22.88 ms | 3481 | 54.7 | 0.984 | 3.61 ms |
| c4 DFly2 | 30.14 ms | 5543 | 50.0 | 0.991 | 3.30 ms |
| c8 DFly2 | 35.41 ms | 7774 | 103.9 | 0.986 | 6.85 ms |

- The plan is step-granular: `Engine::tiering_dispatch` (core/engine.cpp:1021) runs once a step, one
  step behind (engine.cpp:1244), and the next step's plan is made from its per-layer histograms. At
  the start of a pass, layer `l`'s residency is already fixed, so a fork cannot change *what* is
  resident, only *when* a copy crosses.
- W2 (issue #309; core/runtime/w2.cpp, core/runtime/w2.h, arch/common/rad_block_moe_fp8.h) already
  turns a routed layer's exact miss set into copies *inside* the layer: after the launch that holds
  the router's argmax in `mask`, the serving thread posts at most `RAD_W2_MAX_COPY` (8) whole units
  on the prefetch DMA queue, the resident experts' GEMMs run, and the tail (`RAD_W2_TAIL_EXPERTS`,
  16) reads what landed. Its window is `10 + 3·D` us (`rad_w2_kmax`, abi/rad_w2.h:161).
- W2 does not engage below `RADIANCE_W2_MIN_ROWS` (16) rows: at the nospec c4/c8 shapes there is no
  window at all today, and the run's log has no W2 line at shutdown - it planned nothing in 765
  steps. DFly2's verify pass, at 16-40 rows, is where it runs: 6396 routed layers planned, 13 871
  misses, 13 134 units posted of them, 12 186 read from the ring and 948 left to the tail, 0 posts
  dropped (run `s11-df-a`, rank 0, 280 steps).
- So W2 leaves 6.0 misses a step at the DFly2 shapes (948 tail reads plus 737 misses past the copy
  list, 280 steps): 0.20 ms of stall, 0.40 ms of link. That is the entire prize a schedule could
  still win there, against a 21-29 ms fork.
- The cross-layer predictive path (core/runtime/xprefetch.h, M-01/M-02) does not close the gap
  either: Z-08 measured its precision at 23-45 % at these hit rates
  ([tp2t3.md](../refs/notes/tp2t3.md)), which is why this item wants exactness instead of a guess.

## 2. The two schedules, and what each costs

**S1, the whole step split** (the item's "2-microbatch ping-pong"): rows [0, r) and [r, R) with
r = R/2, as two passes on the compute stream, layered so that each half's copies cross behind the
other half's work:

```
A.trunk(l)   # attention at l, router -> A's exact misses -> post the copies
B.trunk(l)   # the duplicate trunk read; A's copies cross behind it
A.moe(l)     # resident experts, then the tail (A's misses have landed)
B.moe(l)     # B's copies crossed behind A's MoE
```

Every layer is forked, so every layer's trunk weights are read twice.

**S2, the per-layer fork/merge** (the item's "merges back into one batch on layers with few
misses"): the step runs merged, one pass of R rows, except on the layers the plan marks hot; there
the layer is issued as in S1 and the next layer merges again. Per forked layer: one duplicate trunk
read, one extra AR pair, the MoE's two half-batches, the launches.

Both fit the existing shapes without a second set of row arrays: the batch's arrays sit at one
address per rank for the engine's life (abi/rad_runtime.h), so a micro-batch is a *view* (base plus
a row offset) and the plugin's chain already takes the row count `T` as a parameter. What a build
would need:

- `RadBatch` carries the split (a second group's row count, or a flag). That trips the
  `static_assert(sizeof(RadBatch) == 512)` at core/runtime/ctx.cpp:1068, which exists to force the
  decision: the field must be mixed into `Ctx::pass_key` (ctx.cpp:1073), because a forked pass
  issues a different launch list from a merged one.
- `Ctx::pass_key` mixes `n_tok` and every row-array pointer, so the split field has to join the key
  too; what that does to the tapes is §5's subject.
- The plugin's chain (`arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash_moe.h`, the loop that issues the whole trunk) issues
  the layers in the forked order; S2 needs no layer-range pass, which is what keeps the tape story
  small (§5).
- W2's mailbox, ring and done words (`RAD_W2_GROUPS`, 3, in abi/rad_w2.h) become per micro-batch:
  two windows a step instead of one, two groups in the ring rotation.
- `Engine::tiering_dispatch` picks the forked layers once a step from the same per-layer histograms
  the plan is already made from, as a pure function of the counts, so both TP2 ranks pick the same
  layers (§6).

## 3. What a miss costs, measured four ways

- M-20 §3: 10-32 us a miss at c1, the marginal cost of the in-place read.
- W2's own A/B at c8 DFly2 (W2-prototype.md §5): the in-layer window removes 3.8 ms of stall a step
  over 111 misses, ~28 us a miss plus ~5 us of tail.
- This lane's copy of that A/B: W2 off at c8 DFly2 (`s11-df-nw2`) is 39.116 ms a step at 140.8
  misses, W2 on (`s11-df-a`, `s11-df-b`) 35.655 and 35.171 ms at 103.9 misses - 3.5-4.0 ms for
  ~140 misses, 25-28 us a miss.
- The link: a unit is 1,499,904 B (891,136 gate_up + 608,768 down), and the trace's own miss bytes
  over its misses give 66 us a miss at 8 rows and up - the full link time, 22.7 GB/s effective,
  which is what any schedule's *copy* must spend.

So the simulation carries two numbers a miss: **66 us**, the link, which bounds what a schedule can
hide, and **34 us**, the stall, what a pass pays for an in-place read once the tail overlaps it.
Hiding a copy completely is worth the stall, never more than the link - and W2 already hides all but
6 misses a step at the shapes where it runs.

## 4. The overlap simulation

Method ([`S-11-tools/runahead.py`](S-11-tools/runahead.py)): per dispatch the tool reads the trace's
per-layer per-expert routing counts (which sum to the pass's rows x top-k, k = 16 here - the sums
say so: 64 counts a layer at 4 rows, 128 at 8) and the tier snapshot, sums the pass's routed set and
its misses, and forms the two halves' miss sets under the hypergeometric model that a half is a
random half of the pass's rows,

    P(expert routed by >= 1 of r rows | c of the R rows route it) = 1 - C(R-c, r) / C(R, r)

The trace does not hold which row went where, so this is an expectation, and it is the generous
direction: it assumes the halves' misses are independent samples of the same routing. The snapshot
says *which* experts sat off the card and the record's own `missed` says *how many* did (the two
drift between snapshots, which are taken every 256 dispatches), so the snapshot's set is scaled to
the record's count. The fork is not modelled at all: it is `2·T(r) - T(R)` from the clean runs'
step times on this lane. The window check is per layer: a half's copy bytes at the link rate against
the other half's layer time, `T(half)/41`. Merge-back (S2) forks a layer when its own stall beats
the uniform share of the measured fork, `fork/41`.

| trace, shape | routed | misses a step | a half | fork | hideable stall | net | layers that fit their window | layers worth splitting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| nospec c4, 255 steps | 2305.6 | 22.68 | 11.94 | +11.98 ms | 0.77 ms | **-11.21 ms** | 41/41 | 0/41 |
| nospec c8, 255 steps | 4014.9 | 22.29 | 9.73 | +11.69 ms | 0.76 ms | **-10.93 ms** | 41/41 | 0/41 |
| DFly2, 167 passes | 6872.7 | 85.89 | 59.28 | +25.00 ms (measured range 20.9-28.9) | 2.92 ms | **-22.08 ms** | 41/41 | 0/41 |

Readings:

- **The fork is one extra fixed pass at every height.** Two separate measurements (2 x c4 - c8 and
  2 x c2 - c4) give 11.3-12.7 ms at the nospec shapes, and the fitted pass, `T(n) = 12.20 + 1.50 n`,
  says the same thing analytically: `2·T(n/2) - T(n) = 12.20` whatever `n` is. The sublinearity of a
  decode pass is the whole story, and it is why S1 loses at any height, in any container, without
  needing a trace.
- **The window is not the limit.** All 41 layers fit their half's copies in the window in every
  shape (363-434 us at nospec, 864 us at DFly2), by an order of magnitude. The item's premise that
  a micro-batch's copies need a wider window than `kmax` gives is right, but the window is not what
  a pass pays for: the tail already overlaps most of the read (34 of 66 us a miss), which is why W2
  recovers 3.5-4.0 ms and not the whole 6.9 ms at c8 DFly2.
- **The merge-back is negative on every layer.** The per-layer fork share is 285 us at nospec c8 and
  610 us at DFly2; the hottest layer of the c8 nospec step (L28) misses 1.08 times a step, worth
  37 us, and the hottest of the DFly2 run (L18) 3.35 times, worth 114 us. A layer would have to miss
  8.4 times (nospec) or 18 times (DFly2) a step to pay for its own fork. So S2 has no layer to act
  on in either container at u3, and the "merge back on layers with few misses" rule degenerates to
  "never fork".
- The traced phases read the shapes a little differently from the counters: colder at c4 (22.7
  misses a step against 16.4, because this run has no c2 phase warming the pool first), warmer at c8
  (22.3 against 27.6), and the DFly2 pool is 85.9 over its multi-client passes (against the
  counters' 50.0 and 103.9 at c4 and c8 - a c4 pass and a c8 pass are different heights, and the
  pool mixes them). The fork is what the verdict turns on, and it is measured on the clean runs.
- The halves' expectation is what the fork would buy if the fork were free: 9.7 of the 22.3 misses
  at c8 nospec, 59.3 of 85.9 at DFly2, land in each half - i.e. two halves together expose slightly
  fewer misses than the merged pass (19.5 against 22.3), because a half routes fewer distinct
  experts. That is the whole overlap the schedule can produce, and it is worth 0.76-2.9 ms.

## 5. Tape replay

- A pass is keyed and its launch list recorded (`Ctx::pass_key` at core/runtime/ctx.cpp:1073; 48
  tapes, `Ctx::kTapes`, core/runtime/ctx.h:648, least recently used out), and a recorded pass is
  played as a whole.
- S1's two halves key apart (`n_tok` and every row-array pointer are mixed in) and are separate
  shapes: played as two passes, today's machinery covers them unchanged - and that is exactly the
  serial schedule, not the ping-pong. The *interleave* is the part that has no expression: a tape is
  one contiguous submission per pass.
- The layer-range form of the tape (S-02's `layer_lo`/`layer_hi`) is refused by the key:
  `if (b->layer_lo != 0 || b->layer_hi != 0) return 0;` (ctx.cpp:1105), because a layer-sliced pass
  resumes from carried state that no recorded launch replays. A per-layer tape cut reopens that; it
  is not the small option it looks like.
- So S1/S2 need one tape per *step*: one `RadBatch` of R rows carrying the split, whose issue order
  interleaves the two halves, keyed by the split field and by which layers forked. The halves' W2
  state (ring slot, done word, group index) is part of what a play must restore, or a play wakes the
  wrong group; `PassTape::w2` and `Ctx::w2_kick` are the hook that already exists for one tape's W2
  launches.
- With that, replay is ordinary: the hazard marks keep ordered dispatches ordered, the copies are
  DMA and are not part of the dispatch set, and the tail's wait on its done word is the handshake W2
  already records and replays. `RADIANCE_AQL_STAMP` and `tests/chain_scalar_audit.py` audit per
  pass, so a step tape keeps its order.
- Cost of the choice: a step tape holds both halves' launches (~2x packets) and the split field
  joins the key, but the tape count and the record/verify/play schedule (2nd sighting records, 3rd
  verifies) are unchanged and no new tape kind appears.

## 6. TP2 collective ordering

- A layer has two collectives at TP2: the attention junction's `ar_rmsnorm_ihc_junction`
  (arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash_common.h:817) and the MoE junction's `issue_all_reduce`
  (arch/hyv4flash/hyv4flash_moe.h, after the routed and shared arms). A forked layer has four.
- `ctx.h`'s lane rule 1: every collective rides the lane the pass issues on, because the all-reduce
  double-buffers its slots on launch-count parity; two ranks that alternate differently pick
  different slots. The second micro-batch must therefore **not** be put on the second lane to
  "overlap" - the lane exists for a single pass's own launches (`rad_lane1_window`), not for a
  second stream of collectives.
- The order must be identical on both ranks. Both ranks compute the same rows, weights and router
  counts after the layer's attention all-reduce, so a per-layer fork decision taken as a pure
  function of those counts is identical on both. A decision taken from timing ("fork if the last
  pass was slow") would deadlock and must be ruled out in the build card.
- The fork doubles the ordered collective count; the measured fork term already carries that, since
  it is inside `T(r)`.
- The last micro-batch may be ragged on an odd `R`; the split must be derived from `n_tok` with the
  same rounding on both ranks, or the collective counts diverge.

## 7. The gate for a build card, and what the card would be

No card on u3 (§0). The gate, in the tool's own units:

    build only if (stall a step the schedule can hide) > (fork) + noise,
    the fork being one fixed pass (12.2 ms nospec, 21-29 ms DFly2 at TP2) and
    one forked layer T(n)/41 (285 us nospec c8, 610 us DFly2).

Read as a miss count: the link-bound reading of the gate needs 177 misses a step at nospec c8 (11.69
ms / 66 us) and 317-438 at DFly2 c8; the stall-bound reading needs 344 and 615-850. The u3 shapes
have 22-28 (nospec) and 104 (DFly2). The item puts u4 at 32-36 ms of miss *link* a step, i.e. 490-550
misses, or 16.7-18.4 ms of stall at 34 us a miss: that clears the nospec fork (12.2 ms) and stays
under the DFly2 fork (21-29 ms), so on u4 the whole-step form is plausibly positive at the nospec
shapes and negative at DFly2. The note does not guess further, because no u4 container exists on this
machine or on `iks`. The measurement that would open the card:

1. a u4 lane (TP2), `coupled03` and `nospec`, phases `c2,c4,c8`, three clean runs a side interleaved,
   plus one stamped trace run per shape;
2. `runahead.py <trace> <probe> --steps c2=..,c4=..,c8=.. --half c4=c2,c8=c4` for nospec, and
   `--pool --min-rows 8 --fork <measured>` for DFly2 (its verify height moves with the draft length,
   so no height selects its phases, and `min-rows` drops the single-client passes of a staggered
   start);
3. a card is opened only if the net is a clear win - 1 ms a step or more, above the run-to-run
   spread measured here: 0.45-0.92 ms at the nospec shapes, 0.49 ms at DFly2 c8, and 4.2 ms at
   DFly2 c4, where the draft length makes steps less reproducible.

If it opens, the card is S2, not S1: S1 loses at every height at u3 regardless of container (§0,
§4), because its cost is a whole extra pass and the pass's fixed cost does not shrink with the expert
container, while the trunk read that dominates it is the same. The card's first step is a
`R4D_OVERRIDE_UNITS` override of the MoE unit with the split field and the fork order
(docs/PLUGIN.md §14), so it can be measured on one card before any libr4d rebuild.

## 8. What this note does not settle

- **u4.** No container here; the projection uses the item's own figure and §7's gate is what decides.
- **The per-layer fork cost is a uniform share.** `fork/41` is the layer's duplicate trunk read plus
  its launches and AR pair, which are uniform across the routed layers; the MoE's half-batch
  inflation is not (it depends on each layer's expert spread), so §4's merge-back net is a bound that
  errs against the schedule. The tool prints the per-half expectation per layer so a reviewer can
  redo it with another assumption; the margin (37 us against 285 us on the hottest layer) is wide
  enough that no plausible inflation closes it.
- **The halves' model is an expectation.** The trace has no row-to-token map, so the halves are
  random halves of the rows. A real split (row 0..r-1 against r..R-1) has whatever correlation the
  prompt order gives it - at c8 with 8 independent sequences the correlation is nil, at c4 with two
  prompts it is not, and a card must measure its actual split rather than trust this table.
- **A DFly2 step's two passes.** The trace holds one record a decode step - 218 records for the 190
  steps and 6 prompt passes of `s11-df-tr` - so what is in it is the verify pass (the one whose
  routing is credited a step behind, engine.cpp:1244), not the draft pass. §4's fork term is a whole
  step (both passes), which is the conservative reading; a card that forked only the verify pass
  would cost less and would need its own measurement.
- **Losslessness is not established by construction.** A half-height pass can land in another bucket
  band (ctx.h's slice rule: a slice must sit above the largest boundary below T) and pick another
  kernel, so a forked pass is not byte-identical *by construction* even though the arithmetic is the
  same. The item is labelled lossless (exact routing, no approximation), so a card must check the
  band first (`RADIANCE_AQL_STAMP`, the selection print) and say which band the halves land in. This
  is the one place where S-11 could lose exactness, and it is why §0's verdict is about cost and not
  about a tolerance.
- **W2's engagement rule is a lever the note leaves alone.** At 4-8 rows W2 does nothing, so the
  nospec c4/c8 shapes pay for all 22 misses a step in place. The reason is the window, not the
  threshold: `rad_w2_window_us` is 10 + 3 us a distinct expert, so a 4-row pass has about 22 us,
  while the shortest list a card can post (`rad_w2_list_us(1)`, the 10 us handover plus one 90.6 us
  copy) is 100.6 us. No list lands inside a 22 us window at any copy budget, so opening W2 there is
  a question for W2's own item (a shorter copy, a list per row), not for S-11.
