# M-16 (per-layer residency floor; exit report figures) — notes

Lane `iks-A` (iks, 2× Radeon AI PRO R9700 `gfx1201`, 210 W, TP2, `hy4-flash-iqr-v4-u3.rad`, host pool
16000 MiB, `RADIANCE_HOME=build/radiance_home`), tree: this card's commits `df8df0e4` (the exit
report), `50b326b4` (the floor replay), `ea354019` (the probe phase) and these notes.

Workload: one phase, `longctx` — eight ~46K-token prompts sent one token each, then the same eight
prompts decoded 128 tokens: 8 sessions at 46K context at once (the N8 48K shape of
`refs/notes/longctx.md`, where the stager's ring overflows). Every run recorded
`RADIANCE_ROUTE_TRACE=runs/iks-A/<tag>/trace.rt`, so the same routing could be replayed on the host.

```
scripts/backlog/lane.sh iks-A 'export RAD_LANE=iks-A && scripts/backlog/run.sh m16-after nospec.yaml \
  longctx RADIANCE_ROUTE_TRACE=$PWD/runs/iks-A/m16-after/trace.rt'
build/bin/rad-heat-replay trace.rt [--from 1276 --to 1401] [--layer-min N]
```

## What was built

- `Mover::report()` (`core/place/mover.cpp`): the promotion line said `staged` for `stats_.prefetched`,
  the units heat copied into a slab slot. A long-context prefill stages terabytes through the prefill
  stager and prefetches nothing, so the exit log said `0 staged` in every long-context run. It says
  `prefetches` now, and a `stager:` line follows it whenever the stager ran (`stage_layers ||
  stage_overflow`): the routed layers, the units staged, their bytes, the copies, and the units no
  buffer had room for (read in place) — the figure M-04/E-S2 is about.
- `rad-heat-replay --layer-min N`: replays a recorded trace with a per-layer residency floor. Every
  layer is raised at the start to the highest floor the slab can hold on all of them, never above N
  (the units come off the layers above it, the fullest first, highest expert index first, so the
  slab does not grow); a floor the slab cannot hold is clamped, not overshot. A swap whose demotion
  would take a layer under the floor is abandoned through `heat.abandon` and counted. The replay
  prints `residents a layer: start .. end ..` (min..max, median, sd) always and a `floor:` line with
  the achieved floor and the swaps it cost.
- `scripts/backlog/probe.py longctx`: the phase above, the eight prompts built once so the second
  wave carries the same strings.

## What a per-layer floor costs

Decode window of the `m16-after` trace (`--from 1276 --to 1401`: the 126 dispatches after the last
prefill step of the second wave, when all eight sessions are at ~46K and decoding), 42 layers × 512
experts, 15,415 resident units, 656 routed units a dispatch. Replay of the recorded routing, mover
parameters as the server ran them:

| `--layer-min` | achieved | residents a layer at the start | hit | swaps abandoned |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| off | — | 273..468 (median 379, sd 48) | 0.9676 | — |
| 300 | 300 | 300..453 (379, 45) | 0.9666 | 0 |
| 350 | 350 | 350..401 (379, 23) | 0.9576 | 0 |
| 375 | 375 | 375..377 (376, 1) | 0.9304 | 7670 |
| 400 | 375 (clamped) | 375..377 (376, 1) | 0.9304 | 7670 |

So a floor at the low end of the audit's spread is nearly free: 300 a layer costs 0.1 points of the
decode hit rate (21.2 → 21.9 missed units a dispatch). 350 costs 1.0 point. The slab holds at most
375 a layer at this context (15,415 units over 42 layers), and there the floor is a freeze: no layer
is above it, nothing may be demoted, and 7,670 proposals are abandoned for 3.7 points. At the end of
the same window the unfloored run is at 279..409 (median 392, sd 33): heat's own steady state is
already close to flat, which is why the small floors cost so little.

Over the whole trace (1,402 dispatches, the prefill's included) the replay's hit is 0.8997 while
the rows' own `live_missed` gives 0.7925 — the replay reproduces heat's arithmetic but not the
mover's step, so treat it as a model with a few points of its own error, and compare floors within
one replay, not against the live number. On the decode window alone the two are close (0.9676 replay
against 0.9784 live).

## What a floor does not buy: staging fit

The audit's reason to want a floor was the stager's ring: the heavy pooled layers are the ones that
overflow it (M-04). The replay says the floor goes the other way. The units for a thin layer come off
the layers above the floor, that is, off exactly the heavy layers the ring is short of: the floor at
350 takes up to 118 units off the heaviest layer and gives them to the lightest. A fuller layer
stages less (its routed experts are already resident), so lowering it raises its staged span.

The size of the thing the floor would have to buy is in the run itself. A unit is 1.43 MiB (1739.29
GiB over 1,245,109 units); the ring the stager spreads over is 261 MiB, 182 units. In the decode
phase of the after runs 38,970 to 76,480 units — 54 to 107 GiB of routed experts — had no buffer room
and were read in place, against 472,837 to 646,419 units staged. That is a per-layer span question
(M-04/E-S2) and a residency floor does not touch it; the exit report's new overflow figure is what a
fix has to move.

## Decisions

- **The floor is a raise plus a refusal, not a mover feature.** The card asks what a floor would cost;
  the replay models the cheapest possible mover (raise the start, decline the demotions that break
  the floor, same slab) so the number is a lower bound on the cost of any real implementation.
- **Clamp, do not overshoot.** A requested floor the slab cannot hold on every layer is clamped to the
  highest one it can (a bisection on `sum max(0, res - F) >= sum max(0, F - res)`), because the
  alternative — enforcing a floor no layer can reach — freezes every swap. `--layer-min 400` on this
  trace reports `achieved 375` and runs as 375.
- **Decode window.** The probe's second wave missed the prefix cache (`cache 0` in the request log),
  so the `longctx-decode` phase prefilled again; the decode window is therefore taken from the last
  prefill dispatch of the second wave (1276) to the end, not from the phase boundary. The sessions
  are at ~46K throughout that window, which is what the floor question needs.
- **The per-layer spread to compare with.** The audit's 255–447 (sd 69) was measured on another run;
  this run's own exit report says `resident units a layer: min 1 max 489 mean 390.8 sd 76.4`, and its
  decode window starts at 273..468 (sd 48). The `min 1` is dense layer 0, which holds one unit for
  all of its experts (E-M7's observation); it is left alone here.
