# S-01b (`--decode-headroom`, FP-3): CLOSED — S-01(a) already took the served config's remainder step, so the flag only adds arena rows

Item as written (backlog S-01(b)): FP-3 `--decode-headroom` (dartree `15434c6b`, 9 conflict files incl.
`engine_bringup.cpp` arena sizing vs lending), the general form of S-01: a step that carries a prefill chunk keeps the
whole budget and the decoders' rows ride on top of it, so a newcomer's chunk ends on the checkpoint interval instead of
a 16-48-row remainder step arming its own interleave run. The item claims arrival TTFT under load -27 % at one decoder
(3.25 -> 2.37 s), -14 % at four (2.83 -> 2.43 s), lone prefill unchanged. Verify: `RADIANCE_LOG_STEPS=1` at one and four
decoders with a 6-7K arrival, steps per interval 2 -> 1; `probe.py stall1,stall4`; `sched_test` interval expectations.

The cherry-pick (`a74adc13`), its `sched_test` pin (`3c3dd6c5`) and its docs (`8a82fa0d`) are reverted: the flag is
right, but against the state this branch merges onto it has nothing to make faster, and it is charged to the arena.
All three stay in the branch's history (`git show a74adc13`), and the measurement harness the card needed
(`f5f7f9fa`, `996510ce`: `probe.py ... greedy`, `steps.py`) stays, because the merge bar's first clause wants it on
every card.

## The number

Five states, on the item's own verification: a ~5.7K arrival beside one and four decoders, `RADIANCE_LOG_STEPS=1`. Each
row is one lane, so no comparison here crosses lanes.

| state | arrival's steps beside four decoders | arrival TTFT, 1 / 4 decoders | lane |
|---|---|---|---|
| S-01(a) as merged and served: batch 2048, `checkpoint-interval: 1984` | one step an interval, 1984-row chunks | 2.205 / 2.405 s | iks-A, S-01's own card |
| pre-S-01: batch 2048, interval 2048 | `MIXED 2036 \| 20 \| MIXED 2036 \| 20 \| MIXED 1780` | 2.621 / 2.756 s | iks-A |

Rows two and three are the item's gain, and it is real: at 2048 rows the flag deletes the remainder step, and 2.621 ->
2.229 s at one decoder is the item's -15 % on that lane (it claimed -27 %). Rows four and five are the state this branch
merges onto, and the flag changes nothing there. `checkpoint-interval: 1984` under a 2048-token batch leaves 64 rows of
quantum that the decoders' rows already fit in, so a chunk beside them is 1984 rows and ends on the interval with or
without the flag: the remainder step S-01(b) removes is the one S-01(a) had already removed. `sched_test` pins it
(`decode_headroom_leaves_the_served_interval_schedule_alone`: identical chunk vectors, identical step token counts), the
six lane runs below show it, and the arena grows anyway — 64 rows of headroom, `step_tok` 2112 at the 2048 budget,
**+43.34 MiB a rank** (1.39 -> 1.43 GiB; the bring-up's own line; 43.34 MiB = 30 units = 0.14 points by the backlog's
100 MiB = 70 units, of which the tiered placement lends back what a small step does not use).

## Why it closes without code

The merge bar (§2) wants "the kernel, op or step the item targets measurably faster in ms" than the state it merges
onto. The step S-01(b) targets is not in the served schedule any more, so there is no A/B against S-01 in which this
flag is faster; and `auto` is the default, so the served TP2 and TP4 configs would pay the 64 arena rows and 43.34 MiB
a rank on every card for a schedule that does not change. The tiers lend most of it back while the steps are small,
which is why this is not called a residency loss here — but it is not a saving either, and nothing on the served
schedule asks for it.

That is what the item's own note said it would find: "(a) config ... (b) FP-3" are one fix on either side of the
batch, and (a) landed first.

## The other reading of the review: drop the interval

It was open to this card to delete `checkpoint-interval: 1984` from the served configs so the headroom delivers at the
full 2048 rows. It is not done here:

- the served arrival TTFT at the two settings is equal inside noise (2.205 / 2.405 s served against 2.229 / 2.426 s at
  2048 with the headroom, iks-A, TP2, u3, three runs an arm, interleaved), so the swap buys no ms — it would be
  S-01(a)'s merged decision undone for a wash plus 43.34 MiB a rank;
- the earlier reading that the interval is the looser half of the pair came from comparing the **local** lane's 1984
  arms (2.08-4.25 s at one decoder over thirteen runs) with the **iks-A** lane's 2048 arms (2.23-2.31 s). Two lanes,
  not two settings: this note does not use it, and nothing here says the interval is worse than the full batch;
- it moves the text of every prompt longer than one chunk off the 1984 chunks S-01(a) merges, for no measured gain.

If a later card wants the interval gone, it is a config-only card with its own same-lane A/B (three runs an arm, both
orderings), and it can cite this note for why the flag would then be in the same place S-01(a) is: one step an
interval, no remainder step.

The one shipped config where the served fix is *not* in place is `deploy/compose/tp1.radiance.yaml` (flashnext on one
card: `max-num-batched-tokens: 4096`, `checkpoint-interval: 4096`, four sequences, three draft tokens, so a step beside
the decoders is 4096 - 16 rows and a 16-row remainder step carries the checkpoint). The fork's answer there is the same
config-only one S-01(a) used — a quantum under the batch, ~4032 — not a 43.34 MiB arena level. That is another card's
file and another card's lane; it is recorded here and left alone.

## The lane

This card's evidence is a served A/B, so it was taken on a lane, at the config that is served:

- **local lane** (TP2, `gpu2`, cards 0 and 1), the served config `scripts/backlog/coupled03.yaml`
  (`max-num-batched-tokens: 2048`, `checkpoint-interval: 1984`, `max-num-seqs: 8`, `num-speculative-tokens: 5`), three
  runs an arm around one hour, `RADIANCE_LOG_STEPS=1`, phases `stall1,stall4,greedy`, tags `s01b-1984-<arm>`. The off
  arm is the same file with `decode-headroom: 0` in the model block: the 2.0 server takes its settings from the YAML
  only (a `--decode-headroom 0` on the command line is refused), so the arm is a config line, and everything else is the
  same build and the same lane.
- **iks-A** (TP2, u3), the 2048-row arms and the pre-S-01 baseline in the table above, three runs an arm, interleaved,
  which is where the item's gain shows; `a74adc13` and `8a82fa0d` carry the run detail.

Three steps for the arrival, one an interval, in every run of both arms: the served config already has the schedule the
item asks for, and the flag adds no step to remove. The rows a step carries move by a couple of tokens from run to run
(1988-1990, 2000-2008) with the decoders' own rows, not with the flag, and the four greedy prompts come back with the
same sha256 (`9f77180a…`, `285d021c…`, `d3d2aa2a…`, `e5c67cf1…`, the 5.7K arrival included) in all six runs.

Arrival TTFT: 2.424 s (one decoder) and 2.552 s (four) with the headroom, against 2.539 s and 2.569 s without — the arms'
own spreads at one decoder are 2.309-2.500 s (`on`) and 2.495-2.600 s (`off`), so the difference is inside the noise of
this lane and the note does not read it as a gain for either arm. The arena is the one thing that moves with the flag:
1.43 GiB a rank against 1.39 GiB (`I decode headroom: 64 rows on a 2048-token budget, steps of up to 2112 tokens; the
activation arena is 1.43 GiB a rank, 43.34 MiB more than at the budget alone`).

A seventh run, on the tree this note leaves (the flag reverted, so neither arm), served the same config through the same
phases: `MIXED 1990 | 1990 | 1733` for the arrival, three steps an interval again, 2.453 s at one decoder and **3.168 s
at four** — 0.6 s above the `off` arm at the same config. That spread belongs to the served config on this lane, not to
the flag, and it is why the 43.34 MiB buys nothing here: nothing in these seven runs is distinguishable from it.

## What a later card needs

1. A served config where the batch and the interval are equal, or the interval line gone from the TP2/TP4 configs —
   i.e. a state in which a step beside the decoders really is cut to the interval. Today only
   `deploy/compose/tp1.radiance.yaml` is such a state, and its config-only fix is cheaper.
2. The A/B on one lane, both arms the same build, the flag the only difference (`decode-headroom: 0` in the model block
   against the default `auto`), three runs an arm, alternating, `RADIANCE_LOG_STEPS=1` for the step log and
   `probe.py ... greedy` for the text. Byte-identity is expected at a fixed batch: the flag moves decode rows, not chunk
   boundaries.
3. The arena delta read off the bring-up ("decode headroom: N rows on a ... budget"), charged at 70 units a 100 MiB,
   against the ms the step log shows.
