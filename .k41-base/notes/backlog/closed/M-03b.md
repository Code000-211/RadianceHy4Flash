## Why the 128-row floor loses what it wins

A step of 250-380 rows routes 16 of 512 experts per row, so an expert is hit with p ~= 98 % and the ring copies
almost the whole pooled set onto the card before the layer runs: the item's own figure is 3.7-4 GiB a rank, and at
host-link bandwidth (~40 GB/s) that is ~90 ms. The GEMM saving at that size is 23 ms (table above: 79.1 → 55.7). The two do not cancel, and
the copy does not appear in the op table at all — which is why the 256-row step's table total moves by 1 ms
(164.1 → 162.9) while its TTFT moves by 90 (0.144 → 0.235 s): the ~90 ms is the copy, spent before the pass, on the
critical path of a request whose pass is only ~165 ms long. That is the whole regression, on both lanes, in both runs
each.

The arm pays only once the pass is long enough to amortise the copy, and only if the ring still holds what the step
routes. The 720-row tail chunk of `pf6` is the case where it does: chunk 4 comes after three 1,984-row chunks that
already pulled most of the pooled set into the ring (the before arm's `stage_layers` is 123 when it reaches it), so its
copy is cheap and its GEMM saving is 104 ms — the -77..-86 ms the item promised the 6.7K prompt, and -103 ms on
`iks-A`. The lone 723-row prompt (`tail2560`) has no such history: it copies from cold, and -12..-14 ms is all that is
left of the same 54 ms GEMM saving.

## What a revised item needs

- An arm floor near where the copy pays: on these two lanes that is somewhere between 256 rows (+90 ms) and 720 rows
  (-109 ms of table time, -86 ms of TTFT once warm). A floor at ~500-700 rows would keep the `pf6` win and drop the
  resume-turn loss, but it would also drop most of the case the item is named after ("~128-1024 rows").
- Or a cheaper copy: only the experts that are not already resident, paced over the layers *before* the step rather
  than inside it, and a ring that stays warm across a session's steps — the mechanism that makes chunk 4 win.
- The measurement to trust for the next attempt is a step's own routed-GEMM ms and its own TTFT, not the run's; both
  are one command away now (`prof-steps.py`, and the step log's `prefill n_tok=` lines).

## Seen and left alone

- The item's part (a) — the 1024/1025 off-by-one in the scheduler's busy-chunk floor — is not this card's and is
  untouched; the stager's floor is still `stage_rows / Q * Q` at 1024.
- A workload that is one 6.7K arrival followed by churn turns wins 86 ms once and loses 50-90 ms a turn on this
  branch; the item's stated gain for resumed turns has the sign backwards on both lanes. If the churn case is worth
  having, the copy has to leave the critical path first — that is the revised item, not this one.
- `stage_layers` is a *cumulative* count since the server started, not a gauge of the current step, and it counts
  layer-stages: 41 a staged step here, 82 for the lone 723-row step, which this note does not explain. `/stats` is fine
  once that is known; naming it in the field's docs would save the next card the confusion, and is not this card's
  business.
- The `iks-A` pair was run after first, before second (one run each): the cold 138 GB model read penalises whichever
  arm goes first, so `pf6`'s -103 ms there is if anything understated and the 256-row +31 ms understated too. Two runs
  each arm on `local` are the numbers the verdict rests on.
